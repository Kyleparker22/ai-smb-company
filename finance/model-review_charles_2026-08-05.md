> **Method note.** This review applies the discipline standard of academic valuation practice: a number without a story is a guess, and a story without numbers is a wish. Every input must trace to an explicit value driver (what would have to be true in the business for this cell to be right), every unvalidated assumption must be named as one, and outputs are presented as scenario ranges, never single-point pretend-precision. House rules applied throughout: no fabricated precision, every estimate marked as an assumption, no invented revenue.

## Verdict

**The June-13 model is not the conservative floor the business plan says it is — it is a second, undocumented, far more aggressive story that contradicts both the plan and reality, and it contains zero connection to the company's actual finances.** Business-plan §8 presents ~$240k Y1 / ~$7.0M Y5 ARR as "the floor"; the xlsx, which the plan claims will "update with it," outputs **$780k Y1 / $25.9M Y5** — 3.3x and 3.7x the plan's own headline — off a $2,500 Y1 blended retainer nobody wrote a story for. Meanwhile the workbook has no cash cell, no burn line, no runway, no retention sensitivity, no capacity logic, and no acknowledgment that actual MRR is $0. Direction-not-forecast is only honest if the direction traces to stated assumptions; before today's update, almost nothing in this file did.

## Findings

1. **Two irreconcilable stories, and neither document knows it.** Plan §8 models $850 blended / ~24 clients / ~$240k ARR Y1 as the per-employee floor. The xlsx models $2,500 blended / 26 clients / $780k ARR Y1. $2,500 is neither the per-employee floor nor a stated OS mix — it is an unexplained midpoint with no driver behind it. The plan's caveat ("the xlsx updates with it") describes an update that never happened. A reader diligencing both documents concludes the finance function isn't reconciled — which, until today, was true.
2. **No cost reality anywhere.** Y1 opex is a hardcoded $90,000 with no bridge to the actual books. Real confirmed fixed burn is **$614.22/mo** (~$7.4k/yr run-rate) as of 2026-08-05 — restated +$200/mo on 08-03 when the Claude Max subscription surfaced off-book. The model predates that restatement, the Max line itself, and the July payment failures (Hostinger, Descript). $90k opex implies salaries/marketing that have no stated hiring or spend plan — a number without a story.
3. **No cash, no runway.** `runway.md`'s #1 carried blocker — cash on hand TBD from the Founder — is not even represented as an input cell. A model of a pre-revenue company that cannot express "how many months until zero" is missing its most decision-relevant output. Runway is trivially computable the moment the Founder supplies one number; the workbook never asked for it.
4. **Retention 82%→88% asserted at n = 0, rising each year, with no sensitivity.** Brett's 2a names this the single most likely-wrong assumption; the model not only asserts it but *improves* it annually with no mechanism stated. No cell showed what 65% does to Y3/Y5. (Now added — and see finding 8 for what the sensitivity actually reveals.)
5. **26 new clients in Y1 with no capacity logic.** The white-glove-first-5 rule plus two principals (one with an undefined lane, per the pending admission) supports roughly 11–16 Y1 clients under stated assumptions — and that assumes the autonomy bet is proven by about client 5. The model's 26 embeds that bet as already won. (Brett 2c.)
6. **The model predates the current company.** No OS-tier pricing (Core→Command bands, `pricing/v0/os-tiers.md`), no partner admission, no all-industries warm-first targeting, no Max subscription, no API-dark incident (API disabled since ~Jul 30; runtime loops dead since Jul 28 — the variable-cost line is currently $0 because the machine is off, not because it's cheap).
7. **The model's own outputs contradict the plan's stated margin.** Plan §8 states ~80% gross margin; the xlsx computes 92–94% (infra $200/mo against a $2,500 retainer). Nobody reconciled the two. Either the absorbed-cost assumption or the retainer assumption is wrong somewhere, and the discrepancy went unnoticed for seven weeks.
8. **Retention sensitivity exposes an acquisition-driven model.** Dropping retention 85%→65% cuts Y5 ARR only ~18% in this model — because heroic new-client adds (230 in Y5) mask the leaking bucket. The honest reading is not "retention barely matters"; it is that the model's growth is carried almost entirely by unvalidated acquisition assumptions, and retention risk is being laundered through them. In a realistic capacity-constrained scenario, retention is the dominant driver — which is exactly why it stays flagged as the #1 unvalidated assumption.
