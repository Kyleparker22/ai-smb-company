# Operating agreement — placeholder

**No template is provided here on purpose.** An operating agreement allocates ownership, control,
distributions, and what happens when someone leaves or underperforms. A generic one copied out of a
repo is the wrong instrument for that, and the failure shows up years later when it matters most.

**Single member?** Many states do not require one. Write a short one anyway — it is part of what
separates you from your company in a dispute.

**More than one member? Get counsel.** The specific things that bite:

- **Control at 50/50.** Equal splits mean neither party can decide anything alone. That is a choice,
  not a default — make it knowingly.
- **Vesting.** Without it, someone who leaves in month two keeps their full stake forever.
- **Defined duties.** "Substantially full time" against no written duties means a service-failure
  clause can never fire — you cannot judge a standard nobody wrote down.
- **Funding the buyout.** Survivors usually cannot pay; an estate usually cannot wait. Insurance is
  the common answer and it has to be arranged in advance.
- **Tax treatment of grants.** Profits interests, §83(b) timing — see `valuation-worksheet-TEMPLATE.md`.

Record counsel's review with `counsel-review-TEMPLATE.md`, and track every blocked item in
`processes/counsel-gates.md`.
