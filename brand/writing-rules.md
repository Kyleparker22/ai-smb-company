# Writing rules — how yourco writes a sentence

> Canonical anti-slop writing rules. Owner: **Luka** (brand custodian). Read at Step 0 by **Reilly** (outbound),
> **Katie** (content), and applied by **Melanie** (the assistant). This is the sentence-craft layer; the
> positioning layer (Always/Never, sentence patterns) lives in `brand/v0/brand-guidelines.md → Voice & tone`.
> Don't keep a second copy of these rules anywhere — point here instead.

Write like a person talking to one other person. If you wouldn't say it out loud, don't write it. These are
defaults, not laws. The read-aloud test below overrides any single rule when a real person genuinely would say
the thing.

One named exception: the blessed sentence patterns in `brand/v0/brand-guidelines.md → Voice & tone` (for
example "We don't sell tools. We deliver employees.") are deliberate, load-bearing taglines, not filler
negation. Keep them. The ban on "not X but Y" is about throwaway elevation, not your chosen lines.

## Words and phrases we don't use
- "delve into" / "unpack" → "look at," "break down"
- "this signals that" / "this underscores" → say what's happening and what to do about it
- "navigate the complexities of" → name the actual problem
- "in an ever-changing landscape" → delete it, it says nothing
- "synergies" / "leverage our learnings" / "holistic approach" → say what worked, what didn't, what's changing
- "moreover" / "furthermore" / "additionally" / "that said" / "importantly" → don't open with a transition word you wouldn't say in conversation
- "foster a culture of" / "lean into" / "powerful opportunity" → corporate-therapist voice, cut it
- "ultimately" / "at the end of the day" / "it comes down to" → if the conclusion fits any company on earth, it's not a conclusion
- "it's worth noting" / "here's the thing" / "the reality is" / "the truth is" → throat-clearing, just say the thing
- "not X but Y" / "instead of X, Y" as a forced negation → say what it is
- "isn't just X, it's Y" → same elevation trick, cut it
- "not this, or this, or this, but that" → staccato repetition is an AI tell
- "designed to" / "helps you" / "empowers" → say what it does
- "quietly underscores" / "subtly highlights" → adverb padding, say "this matters"
- "seamlessly" / "effortlessly" / "robust" / "cutting-edge" / "game-changer" / "transform" / "supercharge"
- rule-of-three filler — everything arriving in threes because three sounds finished ("faster, cheaper, better")

**Conversational agents only (Melanie):** "great question," "I'd be happy to," "absolutely," "I hope this helps."
Outbound and content don't run into these; the assistant does.

## Formatting
- The **em-dash is our worst tell.** Cap it: at most one per paragraph, and only where a comma or period won't do.
- No "**Bold word:** explanation" bullet pattern.
- Don't open every paragraph with a transition word.
- No neat bow at the end that could close any email.
- Keep bullets to a minimum. If it fits in a sentence, write a sentence.
- No bold scattered through paragraphs for emphasis.

## What's prose, what's display copy
These rules govern **prose** — email, posts, memos, briefings, this kind of writing. **Display copy** runs on
fragments and has its own rhythm: carousel slides, headlines, CTAs, captions. A slide reading "Books the
appointment. Answers after hours." is right even though it's not a full conversational sentence. Don't force
display copy through the talk-to-one-person test.

## The test
Read it out loud. If you wouldn't say it in conversation, rewrite it. If you can't summarize a paragraph's point
in plain English, delete it — it sounded smart and said nothing. If your conclusion would copy-paste into any
other company's email and still fit, it's too generic. Add the name, the number, the example.

## How to write instead
- Short sentences. Say what you mean.
- Normal words. "Look at," not "delve into." "Because," not "given the fact that."
- One idea per paragraph. Make the point and move on.
- Be specific. Names, numbers, dates, examples. Vague writing is bad writing.

## Slop → human (yourco voice)
**Slop:** "YourCo leverages cutting-edge AI to seamlessly transform your front desk into a 24/7 powerhouse."
**Human:** "YourCo runs your front desk with an AI employee. It books appointments and answers after hours. You
approve anything that goes to a customer."

**Slop:** "Our holistic, eval-driven approach empowers businesses to navigate the complexities of AI adoption."
**Human:** "Most AI agents break in production. We run the evals, the approvals, and the monitoring so yours
doesn't. You see every check pass."
