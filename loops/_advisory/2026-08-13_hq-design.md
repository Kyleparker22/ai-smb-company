## Part 1 — the HQ landscape, and what is worth taking

Researched 2026-08-13. **The current object under review:** 9 doors, 23 view sections,
**65 panels**, 2,297 lines of `index.html`, 15 Python modules. That scale is itself the finding
most of this table circles.

| Source | The idea | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Amazon's WBR** — 400–500 metrics in 60 minutes, *identical layout every week*, and the **6-12 chart** (trailing 6 weeks beside trailing 12 months, same metric) | The mechanism is not the metric count, it is the **unchanging format**: anomalies pop because the eye already knows where everything sits. Nobody hunts | **STEAL — the strongest item.** Every HQ panel renders *now*. There is no trailing view anywhere, so nothing can look anomalous — a number can only look like itself. `timemachine.py` can already compute any metric as of any date, so the raw material exists and the missing piece is a strip, not an engine |
| **Amazon's input-vs-output discipline** | Executives manage *controllable inputs*; outputs are the score, not the lever | **STEAL, and it is embarrassing.** All **nine** goal metrics are outputs — mrr, liveClients, dealsInMotion, newProspects, activeConnectors, prospectiveConnectors, activeAdvisors, referredMRR, marginPct. **Zero are things the Founder can do on a Tuesday.** See Idea 1 |
| **"Alerting over browsing"** (Datadog/Grafana consensus, 2026) — *most dashboards should be linked to by alerts*; browsing is directed | HQ is a pure browse surface. HQ already has hash routing and filter state; nothing generates a link into it |
| **Dashboard fatigue** (the same literature) — too many surfaces without prioritisation sends people back to intuition | 65 panels, built by one person, read by one person | **Confront it, don't add to it.** This is N1 below: HQ should be the thing that tells you which of its own panels is dead weight |
| **Tufte — data-ink, one question in 5 seconds, 3–7 KPIs above the fold, exception-first** | Remove decoration; the primary question should be the largest thing on the page | **Mostly already ours.** HQ is dark, dense, low-chrome, and "Today" leads with needs-you. | **Bloomberg Terminal** — density + keyboard-first + a compact learnable grammar for expert navigation; boring beats pretty for a daily expert tool | The Terminal's users tolerate ugliness for speed and predictability | **Trigger-gate.** A ⌘K palette over 9 doors × filters is real value, but it is navigation polish for a surface with one user who knows where everything is. **What not to copy:** the genre's instinct to add a pane per capability. HQ has been growing a door
per idea — including six of mine today. Tufte's actual lesson is subtraction, and it is the one
principle in this table that HQ is currently violating hardest.

## Part 2 — three ideas HQ has not considered

**Idea 1 — Put controllable inputs on the Goals band, above the outputs.**
*Bezos · Christensen · Munger; escalates CV-C in a new form.* Every one of the nine goal metrics is
an output. On any given Tuesday the Founder cannot move MRR — he can move *conversations started, audits
delivered, proof artifacts shipped, warm intros made, referral asks made*. Outputs are the score;
inputs are the game, and HQ currently shows only the score. The fix is a second row on the Goals
band — 3–5 input metrics with weekly targets, computed from the CRM's activity log rather than
typed in. **Munger's sharper version:** a dashboard of outputs a founder cannot influence this week
is a vanity surface no matter how honest each number is.

**Idea 2 — Make HQ a destination, not an entry point.**
*Martell · the alerting-over-browsing consensus.* HQ's exception signals only fire for someone
already looking at HQ. Invert it: the Monday briefing, the Slack digests and the runtime alarm carry
**deep links into the exact panel and filter** — `#board?state=needs-you&owner=the Founder`,
`#partners?panel=lockin`. The routing and filter state already exist; nothing constructs a URL into
them. This costs almost nothing and converts every existing notification from "something happened"
into "here is the screen about it." **Karpathy's caveat, kept:** a link is not an alert. Something
still has to decide what is worth interrupting for, and that decision belongs with the loops, not
with HQ.

**Idea 3 — The 6-12 strip: two horizons on every headline number.**
*Bezos · Tufte (small multiples).* Trailing six weeks beside trailing twelve months, same metric,
same place, every week. Anomalies become visible without anyone hunting for them, which is the
entire mechanism behind Amazon reviewing 500 metrics in an hour. `timemachine.as_of()` already
computes any metric at any date, so this is a rendering job on an engine that exists. **The honest
limit:** at 0 clients most of these lines are flat at zero, and a flat line teaches nothing. This is
worth building the week revenue starts, not before — which makes it the clearest **Later** on the
list rather than a **Now** dressed up as one.

## Part 3 — three things nobody has built

Novelty bounded: against the dashboards and literature researched today. Not a patent search.

**N1 — The dashboard that audits its own usefulness and argues for its own deletion.**
HQ has 65 panels and one reader. Every dashboard product on earth adds panels; none of them tells
you which of its own panels is dead weight. yourco can, and the machinery shipped today: log two
things per panel — *was it opened* and *did anything on it change since the last open* — and apply
the retirement logic already written for agents. A panel that never changes and is never opened is
proposed for removal, with the evidence. **This is CV-G (subtraction is the only free move) pointed
at HQ itself**, and it is the only idea in this report that makes HQ smaller. *Tufte would say it is
also the only one that makes it better.*

**N2 — Diff-first HQ: "what changed since you last looked" as the landing view.**
Dashboards render state; the question a returning founder actually has is *what moved.* Because
every HQ payload is derived and therefore snapshot-able, HQ can store the payload hash at each
visit and compute a true cross-company diff — not a changelog somebody writes, a computed delta
across the Board, Clients, Evidence, Partners and Goals, rendered as the first thing you see.
"Since Monday: 2 domains slipped · 1 trip-wire fired · Partner B took 3 items · 9 agents proposed for
retirement." That is the alerting-over-browsing principle made **native** rather than bolted on
through Slack. Some products do "since last visit" for a feed; nobody does it as a computed diff of
an entire company's derived state.

**N3 — The prosecution panel: HQ arguing against its own headline numbers.**
Every dashboard is a persuasion surface for the numbers it displays. None ships a built-in
prosecutor. yourco already has the primitive — the CRM's `spread` runs two opposed readers over one
evidence bundle — and nothing points it at HQ's headlines. The panel takes each top-line figure and
states the strongest honest case that it is misleading, computed from the same data: *"Pipeline
$24k — all three deals are past their own median velocity, none has a next action dated this week,
and two have not been touched in 30 days."* The Evidence door proves yourco won't overstate a number
it can't defend; this proves it will argue against a number it *can*. **Munger:** the ability to
argue the other side better than the other side is the only real test that you understand your own
position — and no dashboard has ever been built to do it.

## Did the last run change anything?

**Yes, more than any previous run — and its central warning was overridden.** This morning's
`2026-08-13_ai-os-design.md` produced six ideas and a Now action of "build none of them this week."
the Founder built all six the same day. The builds were sound and found three real bugs, including one
that had silently emptied every agent's activity panel in HQ for weeks. So the *ideas* were good and
the *sequencing advice* was rejected — and the thing the sequencing advice was protecting (the
partner lock-in run) is now 0 of 14 locked with 5 domains past their date.

**Retire test: not triggered, but flagged.** Three consecutive runs have converged on capacity, and
two have been overridden. A panel whose central finding is reliably ignored is not yet useless —
this run produced three new convergences and a Now action that costs an hour — but if the next run
names capacity a fourth time and is overridden a third, the honest recommendation will be to stop
running the panel rather than keep generating advice the company has decided not to take.
