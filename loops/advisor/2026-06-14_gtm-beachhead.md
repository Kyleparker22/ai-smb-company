# Advisor memo — narrow the beachhead before you spread (2026-06-14)

> On-demand memo (Brett). Trigger: a GTM-strategy prompt + a reminder of beachhead theory (Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm*). Short, one recommendation.

## The recommendation
**Concentrate the whole GTM on landscaping + hardscaping (Yourtown/FL metro) until that corner is undeniable. Hold the other ~19 industries.**

## Why
The fastest-growing startups don't boil the ocean — they find the most flammable corner of one market and light it, then let FOMO pull the rest. yourco already *has* the corner and the wedge; the risk is spreading before the corner is won.

- **The corner is already chosen and proven-ish:** landscaping/hardscaping owner-operators losing deals to missed calls and the 1–2 month quote gap. The pricing is locked there (`pricing/v0/landscaping-hardscaping.md`), the demos exist, and **Sample Client is the live wedge** — a real hardscaper, a real use case, a proposal out.
- **The cited competitors prove the lane is enter-able:** Mario's AEO scan (`loops/aeo-geo/2026-06-14.md`) shows the vertical answer-space is point voice-receptionists with per-vertical pages — beatable with yourco's *operated, approval-gated* employee + real proof.
- **The current outbound is too broad for the stage:** the engine is built for 20 industries (`processes/outbound/industry-campaigns.md`). Pre-revenue with one wedge client, that's diffusion. Beachhead logic says win one trade so completely that a handful of installers would cry if you took it away — *then* the case study makes every other Yourtownhardscaper feel behind.

## What this changes (concrete)
- **Reilly:** run outbound into landscaping/hardscaping only until there are 2–3 live references. Park the other vertical campaigns (staged, not sent).
- **Webb/Mario:** per-vertical pages + AEO for landscaping/hardscaping first (already the AEO plan's #1).
- **Delivery:** Sample Client → case study with real numbers (it's already the brotherhood ask) → that's the FOMO asset for the rest of the vertical.
- **The metric that matters:** not logos across industries — depth in one. "How many hardscapers would not survive without us," not "how many industries did we touch."

## The counter-argument (honest)
Warm-network deals will come from other verticals (the CRM has roofing, insurance, real estate, wealth, staffing contacts). Take those — warm intros close regardless. The discipline is about where *cold, deliberate* effort goes: one corner, not twenty. Don't turn down a warm hardscaping-adjacent deal on principle.

## Revisit
When landscaping/hardscaping has 2–3 live, referenceable clients + a case study, open the next adjacent corner (roofing is closest — same buyer profile, same intake pain, pricing extends cleanly).
