## The two that matter — competitors, not tools

| | |
|---|---|
| **Alven.AI** | An **AI real estate agent** for landlords/agents: leasing, maintenance, delinquency, move-out, compliance, accounting. **$500K ARR in 2 months.** |

**This is `Pre Build Ideas/property-management/` (Property OS), shipped and selling.** Same three
loops — maintenance, leasing/turnover, money. Our version is a synthetic-data prototype with zero
clients; theirs has revenue.

**Steal the pattern — one specific mechanism:** *"When leads call, your phone rings first — Alven
only picks up if you miss the call."* That is a **trust ramp**, and it is better than anything in our
prebuild. It inverts the adoption objection: the owner never gives up the call, the AI only catches
what would otherwise be lost. It costs nothing to add and it is exactly the shape of yourco's
autonomy matrix (R1 floor earning up on evidence) expressed as a *product feature a buyer can feel*.
→ **Absorb into `Pre Build Ideas/property-management/BUILD.md` and the intake pillar generally.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Podium** | **"AI Operating System for Home Services"** — AI employee "Larry" answers calls, books jobs, FSM, on-site payment, reviews. **10,000+ SMBs** (per OpenAI's own case study). |

**The category name is taken in home services.** Podium is using yourco's exact positioning phrase
against yourco's exact ICP, at scale. Two consequences, neither of which is "build faster":

1. **The home-services prebuilds face an incumbent**, not an empty field. `demo before Audit` still

2. **The horizontal positioning is now an asset, not a hedge.** Podium is vertical-locked to
   HVAC/plumbing/electrical. yourco's Audit-first, any-industry motion is the thing Podium cannot
   copy without abandoning its own product shape.

→ **Brett's next monthly memo.** This is a moat question, not a tooling question.

## Grok Bot — validation, and a competitive signal

Launched 2026-08-11. Persistent **named** agents with long-term memory, their own cloud computer,
cross-tool access, inter-agent communication, working 24/7 and **"only coming back when something
needs approval."**

Read that sentence next to yourco's own: *named agents · always-on runtime · approval gate ·
autonomy earned per action.* **xAI has shipped yourco's architecture as a horizontal product.**

- **Prior art:** `decisions/2026-07-05_tool-triage.md` §2026-07-20 triaged **Grok *Voice*** and
  trigger-gated it as the named challenger to the Vapi lock. **Grok *Bot* is a different product**
  and this is its first triage.
- **What it does NOT change:** yourco does not sell agents, it sells an operated outcome with a
  reliability layer. Grok Bot is a tool a business would run itself — which is the self-serve
  posture `rejections/2026-06-16_self-serve-saas.md` refuses, and its failure mode (the buyer
  absorbs the eval risk) is unchanged by a better runtime.
- **What it DOES change:** "a team of named AI employees" is no longer a differentiator. The
  differentiator is what remains when a client could just buy Grok Bot — **eval evidence, approval
  gates, audit trail, and someone accountable.** That is the moat thesis, now under live test.
- **Steal the pattern:** their agents **share one cloud machine** — files, browser sessions and
  credentials common to all, with separate roles/screens. yourco's agents share a git repo but not a
  session. Worth watching as the runtime grows; not worth rebuilding for.

→ **Brett's memo, alongside Podium.** Two independent data points that the category is being
entered from above.

## Correction (same day) — Dreamteam CRM, after reading the actual site

The first pass triaged this from a search snippet and skipped it as *"`crm-autolog` + the insight
layer, which we run."* the Founder sent the live URL. **That verdict was wrong, and the reason it was wrong
is worth more than the verdict.**

`dreamteam.co` is an **AI-native CRM built out of five named agents** — Frank (lead qualification),
Rachel (research), Sally (pipeline health), Raj (RevOps), Alex (CRM admin) — with, in their own
framing, *"zero-form data capture"* and **"draft-based AI actions requiring human approval before
execution."** Pre-launch, waitlist, free to start.

Read that last clause again. **Draft-then-approve is yourco's core mechanic** — *"the Founder sends; agents
draft"* — shipped as a CRM product's headline design. And their positioning line is the one yourco
uses about no-code tools: *"traditional CRMs like HubSpot remain form-dependent despite AI additions
… the intelligence is not a feature — it is the entire interaction model."*

**Still not an adoption.** Our CRM is built, dogfooded, and its insight layer does seven things a CRM
row cannot produce. Buying a pre-launch CRM at n=0 clients is the shiny-tools trap.

**But it is the fourth data point in one batch**, and that is now the finding rather than any single
tool:

| Who | Ships yourco's architecture as | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Alven** | named AI agent + approval | property management |
| **Podium** | "AI Operating System" + AI employee | home services |
| **Grok Bot** | named persistent agents, returns only for approval | horizontal |
| **Dreamteam** | five named agents, draft-then-approve | CRM |

**Four independent teams shipped named-agents-with-an-approval-gate inside one quarter.** That
retires "a team of named AI employees who draft and wait for you" as a differentiator — it is now
table stakes, arrived at convergently. What remains is what none of them sells: **eval evidence, an
audit trail, and a company accountable for the outcome.** That is the moat thesis, and it is now
under live test rather than argued.

→ **Brett's memo, as the batch's headline** — not four separate tool notes.
