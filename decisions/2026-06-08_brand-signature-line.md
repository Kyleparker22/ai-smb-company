# Decision: YourCo's Signature Line — *a learning, I employ.*

**Date:** 2026-06-08
**Owner:** the Founder, advised by Luka
**Status:** ⛔ SUPERSEDED 2026-06-10 — "a learning, I employ." retired in favor of the primary tagline **"We learn your business. AI does the work."** See `/decisions/2026-06-10_brand-tagline.md`. (Was locked at brand v0.1; kept for history.)

## What was decided
YourCo adopts a new tertiary signature line: **`a learning, I employ.`**

The brand now has two complementary signature lines:
1. **`— yourco, in atelier`** (v0, original) — warm, Italian heritage, atelier vibe. For essays, personal readouts, where warmth lands.
2. **`a learning, I employ.`** (v0.1, new) — analytical, wordplay-as-wax-seal, rewards a careful reader. For proposals, exec readouts, the bottom of the website, business card backs, the closing line of a pitch deck.

## The mechanic
**AI** = **A** + **I**. The sentence diagrams the acronym:
- The **A** is *a learning* (the AI agent, which learns)
- The **I** is the human, *employing* the agent
- The **I** also carries the yourco nod — "yourco" is Italian for *I learn*, so the I doubles as the yourco subject

Three meanings layered in five words.

## Punctuation rules (locked)
- Lowercase `a` (matches brand wordmark posture)
- Comma after `learning` — mandatory; without it the parse is ambiguous
- Capital `I` — the only capital letter in the line. The contrast is the wink. Never lowercase.
- Period at end — when surface allows, the period is brass `#B8965A` (subtle, intentional, the small craft moment)
- No italics, no bold

## Where it earns its place
- Email signature footer
- End-of-essay / end-of-post signature
- Website footer
- Business card back
- Last line of pitch deck / proposal
- Inside cover of printed documents
- LinkedIn company bio

## Where it does NOT belong
- Cold email subject lines
- Above-the-fold hero lines
- SMS messages
- Trade-show / booth headlines
- Anywhere a buyer has fewer than 5 seconds to parse it
- Reilly's outbound copy (stays functional, clear)

## Why this matters
The functional hero lines (`Named digital employees. Live in 48 hours.`) tell prospects WHAT YourCo does. The signature line gives careful readers something to discover — the wordplay rewards attention and signals "this is a brand built with intent." It's a craft moment, not a value proposition.

## Revisit conditions
- After 30 days in use across email signatures + LinkedIn + website: does the wordplay land or confuse? If sophisticated prospects quote it back, ship more aggressively. If most people miss the parse, demote it from primary signature to occasional use.
