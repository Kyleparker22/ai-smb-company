# 2026-06-16 — yourco offering: "Company OS" for the boomer-business handoff (ETA / acquisition operator)

## Decision (the Founder)
yourco adds a B2B offering: an **AI operator that runs a freshly-acquired SMB** — captures the departing owner's knowledge, executes the first 100 days, then handles ongoing back-office + operations for a first-time owner drinking from a firehose. Aimed at the **search-fund / ETA / holdco** buyer.

## Why this is the strongest moat-fit of yourco's new offerings
- **It productizes yourco's stated differentiator.** The moat is the proprietary data on *how each specific acquired business runs*, compounding into a **cross-business pattern library** — every business operated makes the next onboarding faster and the playbooks smarter. That's the OtherVenture "cross-system pattern recognition" thesis turned into a product. **A genuine compounding data moat — stronger than per-client work.**
- **It owns an unowned seam.** Every dollar of ETA tooling clusters at *finding/financing the deal* (Searchfunder, brokers, valuation) or at *running an established business* (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Docyt, Every). **Nobody owns the moment of ownership transfer + the 100 days after** — where the new owner is most overwhelmed and the departing owner's knowledge is walking out the door. That gap is the wedge.
- **It's pure yourco model:** B2B, operated, recurring, peer-GTM. More on-thesis than the DTC care offering.

## The wedge (the sharp part): start in acquired professional-services firms
Not HVAC. Lead with **acquired bookkeeping/accounting practices, IT/MSPs, marketing & consulting agencies**, because: (1) knowledge work → an AI operator is software-shaped, not truck-and-tools; (2) the asset being transferred *is* the owner's institutional knowledge — yourco's highest-value capture target; (3) it overlaps yourco's existing boutique-services ICP → not a cold start.

## What it actually is
1. **Knowledge capture at the seam** (the wedge feature — cheap to build, screaming pain, nobody does it): during/after diligence, ingest the data room + SOPs + the owner's brain via agent-led structured interviews → a **living operating manual.**
2. **A first-100-days execution engine** — not a checklist PDF, an **agentic playbook that does the work**: vendor transitions, payroll setup, client comms, renewals.
3. **Ongoing operator OS** — back office (**partner** with Gusto/Every-style plumbing, don't rebuild banking) + the operational core for that vertical (client delivery, scheduling, billing).

## GTM
Peer-to-peer into the searcher/holdco world: **Searchfunder, Acquiring Minds / SMB-Twitter, MBA ETA clubs, holdco operators.** Tight, reachable, constantly-talking community — exactly yourco's non-salesy peer motion. Dogfood angle: capture/operate yourco itself as proof.

## Pricing / the $100M path (Polo)
Platform fee + per-seat + a **"diligence-to-operate" onboarding fee**, ~$2–5k/mo per acquired business. Tens of thousands of SMB acquisitions/yr + the silver-tsunami transfer wave → a few thousand operated businesses at that ACV is a $100M line. *Aggressive version:* run yourco's own holdco on the OS, prove it on real acquisitions, then license — higher-risk, slower.

## Risks
- **Every (every.io) is the #1 threat** — if it drifts down into operations or down-market into SMBs. Built for *net-new founders* today (funnel starts at free incorporation), finance+HR only, no operational core, no transition layer, not vertical. Watch them.
- Fragmented, hard-to-reach market; SMB owners aren't software-native; vertical depth → slow generalization.

## First 90 days (validate before building much)
Pick one vertical (test **bookkeeping firms or MSPs**) → interview 15–20 recent acquirers on the first-100-days pain → build **only** knowledge-capture + the 100-days playbook → **pre-sell 3–5 searchers before writing much code** → partner for finance/HR plumbing, don't rebuild it.
