HQ visual refresh — pick a direction

Three treatments of the same screen, same real numbers (snapshot 2026-08-13). Only the design varies. Mockups only — nothing here is wired to the live HQ.

presented per brand/DESIGN.md §7 — "for hero-grade visuals present 2–3 concrete options before iterating live"

The diagnosis

  • The direction isn't the problem. You picked Executive Terminal in July over Morning Paper and Mission Control. It's on-brand and it still is.
  • It was drawn for ~8 panels and now carries 65, with one component doing every job: 134 .panel boxes · 150 brass headings · 42 tables · 37 bordered notes.
  • Brass lost its meaning. DESIGN.md idiom 1 — one brass detail per surface. It's currently on every heading, badge, tile value and note border.
  • Everything is a box in a box. Idiom 5 — hairline dividers, not boxes. HQ nests panel → grid → tile to show one number.
  • No banding. Idiom 4 — alternate bands so sections read as chapters. Every panel is the same surface on the same base, ~10% apart.

All three are house rules HQ already violates. The fix is compliance, not invention — which is why none of these options is a new look.

Round 2 — the palette (2026-08-13, after the Founder: "still too much of the same colors")

Option 1 fixed hierarchy and kept the palette. The deeper problem: HQ never adopted the brand's actual colours.

  • Ground is #232833 neutral slate — the brand's is #161B33 indigo, a deep blue-violet. Warmer, richer, not grey.
  • Text is #f4f5f9 cold blue-white — the brand's is #F4EFE6 warm cream. That coldness is most of the flatness.
  • Brass is #d2ae74 — the brand's is #B8965A, a deeper bronze.
  • Two light grounds go completely unused — --cream and --parchment — while idiom 4 literally says "alternate cream / parchment / dark-indigo bands." HQ is 100% dark.
  • --oxblood, the reserved accent, appears nowhere.

So the three below hold Option 1's structure exactly constant — same markup, same copy, same numbers — and vary only the palette. Every hex comes from DESIGN.md. None of this is invented colour; it is colour yourco already owns and HQ ignored.

Round 1 — structure (for reference)

Option 1

Disciplined

Executive Terminal, corrected. Brass rationed to one element per view. Three panel tiers — lead (no box), support (card), reference (hairline only). Notes and tables unboxed. Same world, with hierarchy and air.

Chaptered

Everything in 1, plus banded grounds so each door reads as a chapter, the brass tick motif, a lead question per chapter that answers itself in one sentence, a pull-quote for the single most important line, and the one scroll-reveal DESIGN.md already specs.

Dense

Mission Control revisited — the one you passed on in July, worth another look now there's genuinely more data. No cards at all: ruled columns, a ticker strip, status as a 2px left rule, ⌘K, everything above the fold. Bloomberg's trade — ugliness for speed.

Round 2 recommendation: 1C, with 1A as the safe fallback. 1C gives the most visual relief per scroll and keeps the dark identity you chose, and the light bands are where numbers are easiest to read. 1A is the two-hour version if you want to be on-brand before the 8/26 session without rethinking layout. 1B is the boldest and the one I would not ship first — it throws away the terminal feel you deliberately picked in July.

Round 1 recommendation (structure): 1, then borrow from 2. Option 1 is most of the visual gain for a fraction of the work, and it can't go wrong because it only removes things. Option 2's banding and tick are worth adding once 1 is live and you've seen it for a week. Option 3 is the right answer if HQ becomes a thing three partners read daily — but it's the wrong first impression for the 8/26 lock-in session, where Partner B reads it cold.

And the honest one: the biggest visual improvement available isn't in any of these files — it's having fewer panels. Restyling 65 is worth less than cutting to 40, which is what the panel-usefulness audit starts proposing once it clears its 14-day floor.
