For Buyers

The buyer's roadmap, start to keys.

Seven steps, in order. Most buyers go from first call to closing table in 60–90 days once they find the home.

i.
The first conversation

Wants, needs, neighborhoods, timeline, and what a comfortable monthly payment actually looks like — run your numbers with our mortgage calculator first if you like.

ii.
Pre-approval

A short call with a trusted local lender. In this market, an offer without a pre-approval letter is a letter to Santa. Takes a day or two; costs nothing.

iii.
The search

You'll see every MLS match the moment it lists. We tour on your schedule and give you the honest read — including "walk away from this one."

iv.
The offer

Price is only one lever. Due diligence fee, earnest money, timelines, and terms win houses too — we structure all of it to win without over-risking.

v.
Due diligence

Your inspection window. Home, pest, HVAC, radon as needed; we negotiate repairs or credits from the findings. This is where your agent earns it.

vi.
The countdown

Appraisal, loan clear-to-close, insurance bound, utilities scheduled, final walkthrough. We chase every deadline so nothing slips.

vii.
Closing day

Sign with the attorney, funds disburse, deed records — then the keys are yours. Expect about an hour at the table.

What's "due diligence" in a Your State contract?

NC gives buyers a negotiated inspection window bought with a due diligence fee — paid to the seller, nonrefundable, credited at closing. Until the deadline you can walk for any reason and keep your earnest money. Setting the fee and the deadline well is a strategy call we make together, house by house.

How much do I really need for a down payment?

Not 20%. Conventional loans go to 3% down, FHA 3.5%, VA and USDA to zero for those who qualify — and down-payment-assistance programs exist across the Carolinas. 20% avoids mortgage insurance, but waiting years to save it often costs more than the insurance would. Run scenarios with a lender early.

What does it cost me to have a buyer's agent?

Compensation is agreed in writing up front in your buyer agency agreement, and in many transactions the seller's side offers compensation that covers some or all of it. We'll walk you through exactly how it works before you sign anything.

For Sellers

The pre-listing checklist.

Two weeks of light preparation is routinely worth more than the cost of doing it. Here's where we focus before the photographer arrives.

·Declutter — closets, counters, garage; less is more on camera
·Deep clean like it's a hotel — windows included
·Touch-up paint: scuffs, trim, and any bold walls to neutral
·Fix the little things — dripping taps, sticking doors, dead bulbs
·Curb appeal: fresh mulch, edged lawn, cleared walkway, new doormat
·Staging consult with our Accredited Staging Professional — included
·Gather docs: survey, permits, HOA info, utility costs, warranty items
·Plan for pets & valuables during showings
How do you decide the list price?

A comparative market analysis built from actual closed sales around your home — adjusted for condition, lot, and timing — plus what we're seeing at showings this month. Priced right, homes sell faster and for more; our own listings have sold over asking and gone under contract in days.

What happens after we list?

The full production: staging, photography and drone, the cinematic tour, deliberate MLS keywords, and launch everywhere the MLS reaches. Then CSS-scheduled showings with every piece of feedback reported back to you, and strategy adjusted in real time. The step-by-step is on our Services page.

Should we renovate before selling?

Usually no — big renovations rarely return their cost at sale. Paint, lighting, landscaping, and deep cleaning almost always do. We'll walk your home and tell you honestly which short list is worth it.

Can we sell and buy at the same time?

Yes — it's the most common move we manage. Coordinated closings, negotiated possession dates, or a short leaseback bridge the gap. We run both timelines side by side so you're never homeless or paying two mortgages longer than planned.

The 4-week countdown checklist

4 weeks out — book movers or truck; start decluttering; begin a box-a-day.

2 weeks out — USPS mail forwarding; start utilities at the new address; transfer school records; refill prescriptions.

1 week out — confirm movers; pack an "open first" box (chargers, meds, coffee, sheets, tools); defrost the freezer.

Closing week — final walkthrough, funds ready with the attorney, IDs in hand; update your license and voter registration (NCDMV / SCDMV) after the move.

Plain-English Glossary

The words your contract uses.

Due diligence fee

NC: money paid to the seller for the right to inspect and walk away. Nonrefundable; credited at closing.

Earnest money

Your good-faith deposit, held in trust and credited at closing — refundable before the due diligence deadline (NC).

Escrow

Money held by a neutral party — your deposit before closing, or the tax/insurance account your lender keeps after.

Contingency

A condition that must be met for the contract to close — financing and appraisal are the common ones.

CMA

Comparative market analysis — what your home is worth, built from actual nearby closed sales.

Clear to close

The lender's final sign-off. After this, it's just signatures and keys.

Excise / transfer tax

The state's tax on a sale — ST $1 per $500 of price; SC $1.85 per $500 (deed recording fee).

Title insurance

One-time policy protecting your ownership against anything the title search missed. Cheap; worth it.

Appraisal

The lender's independent value check. Comes in low? That's a negotiation — and we've run it many times.

Under contract / pending

Offer accepted, working toward closing. "UCNS" on the MLS means under contract, no showings.

Seller concessions

Costs the seller agrees to pay on the buyer's behalf — a common negotiating lever.

Leaseback

The seller rents the home back from the buyer briefly after closing — handy when you're selling and buying at once.
