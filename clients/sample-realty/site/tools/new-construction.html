Before You Visit a Model Home

Five things builders won't tell you.

i.
The on-site agent works for the builder

They're professional and helpful — and contractually loyal to the seller. Register your own agent on the first visit; walk in alone and many builders will refuse to let representation join later.

ii.
Your representation typically doesn't raise the price

Builder pricing is set by the community's comp sheet, not by whether you brought an agent. You get a negotiator and an inspector-of-record at the table.

iii.
The base price is the beginning of the conversation

Lot premiums, structural options, design-studio selections, and incentive packages (rate buydowns, closing-cost credits tied to the builder's lender) are all negotiable levers — some more than others, community by community.

iv.
The builder's contract is not the standard state contract

Builder agreements are written by the builder's attorneys and favor the builder — deadlines, deposits, delay remedies, and warranty terms all deserve a careful read before signing.

v.
New doesn't mean inspected

Municipal inspections check code minimums, not quality. An independent pre-drywall inspection and a final inspection routinely pay for themselves — and the builder's own punch-list standard only helps if someone holds them to it.
