# Listing Kit Builder — how to use it

**File:** `flyer-builder.html` · **For:** Sample Contact · **Needs:** nothing but a web browser

## How the panel is laid out

The left panel now reads as a sequence, top to bottom:

| | |
|---|---|
| **1 · Your listing** | Switch between listings; New listing, Example, Back up, Restore |
| **2 · What are you making** | The eight documents |
| **3 · The property** | Address, status, price, MLS, facts — and the features-sheet paste for buyer documents |
| **4 · Photos & video** | Upload and label |
| **5 · This document** | Only the fields the document you picked actually needs |
| **✓ Set once** | Firm story, team, pricing, marketing, promise, your details, publishing |

**Save as PDF stays fixed at the bottom** of the panel, so it's reachable wherever you've scrolled to. It changes to **Save this page** on the tour and listing page, and Publish appears beside it once the website is connected.

## Two different documents

The buttons at the top switch between documents that do **completely different jobs**. They're not three sizes of the same thing:

| | Audience | What it does | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Property Flyer** — 1 page | **Buyers** | Sells the *house* — features, facts, improvements | Showings, open houses, the kitchen counter |
| **Listing Presentation** — 3 pages | **Sellers** | Wins the *listing* — who you are, how you price, how you market | The leave-behind after an appointment |
| **Listing Presentation** — 7 pages | **Sellers** | The full book, a page per topic | The listing appointment itself |
| **MLS Description** | **Buyers** | Headline, long and short copy, with live character counts | Pasting into the MLS |
| **Social Posts** | **Buyers** | Just Listed, open house, price improvement captions | Instagram and Facebook |
| **Cinematic Tour** | **Buyers** | Your photos in motion, with room titles | Sending to buyers; embedding on the listing page |
| **Listing Page** | **Buyers** | The property's own web page, tour included | The link you put in every post and email |
| **Launch Checklist** | You | Everything this listing needs and who does it | Working the launch, start to finish |

**All eight come from the same paste.** Drop in the features sheet once, then click between documents — the flyer, the MLS copy, and the social captions all build from it.

Because they're different documents, each one asks for different things — the panel only shows the fields that document actually needs.

## Whose listing is it?

At the top of **Your Standard Content** there's a dropdown for who the listing belongs to. Everyone on the team keeps their own name, title, phone and email — pick a name and **every document re-signs itself**, cover included.

- **Add someone** puts a new agent on the list; fill in their details and press **Save these details**
- Editing the name and saving **renames** that person rather than creating a duplicate
- **Remove** takes someone off the list; listings already made are untouched

Everything else — the firm story, designations, team photo, pricing steps, marketing strategy — is shared by the whole team and only lives in one place.

## Making a property flyer

1. **Open `flyer-builder.html`** — double-click it. No install, no login, no internet needed.
2. **Paste your features & highlights sheet** into the box at the top — the whole thing, exactly as you wrote it. Add the facts on the line below (`5 beds, 5.5 baths, 6,049 sq ft, 3 car garage, 0.44 acres, built 2005` — any order).
3. **Click "Build my flyer."** It reads your sheet and fills everything in — address, sections, bullets, dated improvements, facts — and tells you what it found.
4. **Look it over,** trim where it tells you to, then **Save as PDF**.

## Making a listing presentation

Almost nothing to type. Your firm story, team, designations, pricing steps, and marketing strategy are **already written in** — they're the same for every listing. Per listing you only fill in:

1. **The property** — address and facts
2. **Prepared for** — the sellers' names, which print on the cover
3. **Today's market** — the two months-of-supply numbers. **Refresh these from the MLS before every appointment**; they date faster than anything else in the document.

Then Save as PDF. That's it.

## Saving as PDF

Choose *Save as PDF* as the destination, and set:
- **Margins → None**
- **Background graphics → on** (sometimes called "Print backgrounds")

That's what makes the dark pages print edge-to-edge. Set once and your browser remembers.

## Notes

- **Your standard content is set once.** Firm story, team photo and roster, designations, pricing steps, marketing strategy, your name and contact details — all stored and reused. "New listing" never clears them.
- **Editing your standard content:** open the "set once" sections and change anything. Wrap words in \*asterisks\* to print them in red italic, the way *to closing.* looks on the headline.
- **It remembers your work.** Close the tab and come back — it's still there. Use **Back up** (copies to your clipboard, paste into a note) and **Restore** to move between listings.
- **Bullets and steps:** one per line. For pricing steps and marketing items, write `Bold lead | the rest of the sentence`.
- **Timeline:** one per line, as `2016 | Kitchen remodel`. Leave it empty to hide the strip.
- **The bar above the page** always tells you what fits and what doesn't, before you print.

## Your listings

Every listing saves itself into the list near the top of the panel. Click one to switch to it; the × deletes it. **"New listing"** starts a fresh one without touching the others — and your firm story, team, designations and contact details are shared across all of them, so you never re-enter those.

## Photos

There's one **Photos** panel near the top, and it's the same for every document. Add the photographer's files — all of them at once — and then tell the tool what each one **is**:

- **The dropdown** names the room. It guesses from the filename, so `2304-kitchen.jpg` arrives already labelled.
- **★** makes a photo the cover.
- **◀ ▶** reorder. **×** removes.
- **Caption** is optional and only used to help the social copy point at the right file.

Once they're labelled, each document takes what it needs:

| Document | What it uses |
|---|---|
| **Property flyer** | No photo — typographic by design. Add them anyway; the social posts use them. |
| **3-page presentation** | The cover photo, on page 1 |
| **7-page presentation** | The cover on page 1, plus the next three as a strip on the marketing page |
| **Social posts** | Names the actual file per slide — "Photo 3 — Kitchen" instead of "a photo of the kitchen" |

**Photos are resized on the way in.** Full-resolution files would overflow what a browser can store and your listing would silently stop saving. They're scaled to 1600px on the long edge — plenty for print at flyer size and for anything on screen. If storage does fill up the tool says so out loud rather than failing quietly.

Use only images you own or have licensed. Never portal or map screenshots.

## The fair-housing screen

Both the MLS and Social views run a word check and show the result in a panel at the bottom: green when clear, amber with a list when something is worth a second look — the exact phrase and why it's flagged.

It catches the usual trip-wires: "family" / "kid-friendly", "safe neighborhood", "walking distance to church", "master bedroom", "perfect for", "exclusive", and similar. **A flagged word isn't automatically illegal** — it's a prompt to look again before it goes to the MLS. This is a word check, not legal advice.

## MLS description & social posts

These give you text to copy, not a PDF. Each block has a **Copy** button and a live character count that turns red if you go over the limit.

**Read them before you use them.** They're drafted from your features sheet by a template — they're a strong first draft that saves you the blank page, not finished copy from you. In particular:
- Check fair-housing language before anything goes to the MLS
- Confirm every fact against the listing agreement
- The social posts never state a price — add it yourself once it's confirmed
- Add brokerage identification per ST/SC advertising rules

## Social — the full kit

The Social view gives you a launch week, not a single caption:

- **Just Listed** feed post
- **Carousel** — seven slides, each with the photo to use and the text to lay over it
- **Reel** — a timed shot list plus its caption
- **Story frames** — five, with sticker suggestions
- **Open house** and **price improvement** posts
- **Posting plan** — what to post when, from listing day through under contract

All of it drafts from your features sheet. You still copy, paste and upload — actually scheduling and publishing to Instagram needs a connected account and a server that runs when your laptop is closed, which is the built system, not a file on your computer.

## Saving the tour as a video

On the **Cinematic Tour** view there's **Save as video for Instagram**. It replays the tour onto a canvas and records it to a real **MP4** — the file Instagram, TikTok and Facebook actually accept.

**Keep the tab in front while it saves.** It records in real time, so a 35-second tour takes about 35 seconds, and browsers stop drawing in a background tab — you'd get a frozen video. The button shows a percentage while it works.

Recorded narration is mixed in automatically, and subtitles are burned in if they're switched on.

## Changing how long a shot holds

Each beat in **Narration & subtitles** has a **"Hold this for ___ seconds"** box. Leave it blank and the beat fits itself to the clip and the words. Type a number and it holds exactly that long.

That's the control for "the kitchen needs one more second" — set it, play it back, adjust. No video editor involved.

## Video walkthroughs

The Photos panel takes **video as well as stills** — a phone walkthrough, drone footage, or a videographer's file. Add it the same way; clips show a ▶ badge with their length.

In the tour, a clip **plays as itself** — no camera move added on top, because real footage already moves. Stills keep their slow pan. Give each clip a room name and it gets the same title treatment as a photo.

**On shooting it with a phone:** the phone isn't the limit — a current iPhone out-resolves anything a listing page needs. Five things separate amateur from professional:

1. **Use a gimbal.** ~$100, and it's the single biggest difference. Handheld shake is what reads as amateur.
2. **Shoot horizontal.** Crop vertical afterwards for Reels.
3. **Lock exposure on the interior** before you move, or every window blows out white.
4. **Move slowly**, one direction per shot, heel-to-toe.
5. **Back into the doorway.** Standing too close bends the room with the wide lens.

**Saving a tour with video:** clips are far larger than photos, so a tour with more than about 40 MB of video can't be put into one sendable file. The tool says so rather than writing something that won't open. Past that size, publish it to the website — video belongs on a server, not in an email attachment.

**Where clips don't appear:** the printed flyer and presentations, obviously. If a clip is set as the cover, the printed pages use a still frame from it.

**Aerials:** drone footage drops straight in. It has to be footage you have rights to — a Part 107 licensed pilot if you're hiring, which is standard for real estate. Satellite and map imagery from Google and similar services isn't licensed for listing marketing and can't be used.

## Narration & subtitles

Open **Narration & subtitles** (it appears on the tour and listing page) and press **Draft the script**. It writes one line per beat from your features sheet — the address to open, a line per room, your number to close.

**A line per beat, not one long recording.** That's deliberate: a single voice-over drifts the moment anything changes, while a line tied to its own frame can't. Each beat waits for its line to finish, so a room with more to say simply stays on screen longer. You don't time anything.

- **Read it first.** Any beat the sheet had nothing to say about is marked **needs your words** — it won't put a line about the pond over a photo of the bedroom.
- **● Record** uses your microphone and your own voice. Nothing to install, no account, no cloning. Press it again to stop, **Play** to hear it back, **Clear** to redo.
- Recording is tied to the **Listing Realtor** at the top — whoever is listed records their own beats.
- The reading time shows next to each beat, so you can see it's paced before you record anything.

**Subtitles are on by default.** Most people watch with the sound off — on Instagram and Facebook video starts muted and many never turn it on. With subtitles the tour still works in silence, which is the common case, and the voice is what makes it better rather than what makes it make sense.

### One long walkthrough instead of many clips

If you shoot the whole house as one continuous video, it plays start to finish — nothing is chopped up. Add **chapter markers** to that beat so the captions keep up with the camera:

```
0:00 | Entry | Walk in and the ceiling opens up.
0:42 | Kitchen | Quartzite counters, rebuilt in 2016.
1:15 | Primary suite | Pond views from the bed.
```

One per line, `time | room | what's said`. Minutes and seconds, or just seconds.

### A generated voice

Cloning a voice once and reusing it on every listing needs the connected build, the same as publishing. Recording here works today and needs nothing.

## The cinematic tour

Pick **Cinematic Tour** and it builds itself from your photos — no extra work. The cover photo opens it, the rest follow in the order you set, each with a slow camera move and its room title. It ends on your name and number.

- **Click it to pause**, click again to resume.
- **"Save this page"** gives you one `.html` file with the photos already inside it. No internet, no account, nothing to install — it opens on any computer or phone, and you can email it or hand it to whoever runs the website.

**Every frame is your photograph.** Nothing is generated or enhanced — the camera moves, the house doesn't. That matters: an AI that invents a room invents finishes the house doesn't have, and that's a misrepresentation problem, not a style choice.

## The listing page

The property's own web page — hero photo, the facts, the tour, your features, the photo gallery, the improvements, and a way to reach you. Same "Save this page" button, same self-contained file.

**One honest limit:** the enquiry form opens the buyer's email app addressed to you. That works today and needs nothing. A real form — one that records every enquiry whether or not the buyer finishes the email, and answers it in seconds — needs the page actually hosted and a system behind it.

## Getting a listing onto SampleRealtyteam.com

The website runs every listing off one file — `listings-data.js`. The builder now writes an entry in exactly that shape, so nothing has to be retyped or reformatted.

On the **Listing Page** view, press **Copy the website entry**, then:

1. Paste it at the top of `listings-data.js`, in the section matching its status
2. Drop the photos into `assets/listings/<address-slug>/` as `photo-0.jpg` onward — `photo-0` is the one on the card

The homepage band, the listings page and the property's own page all update from that one entry.

The **address becomes the slug** — `2304 Highland Forest Drive` → `2304-highland-forest-drive` — and it's the same slug the Publish button uses, so the published page and the site's own listing page resolve to the same address rather than two competing ones.

Two fields only the listing page uses: **List price** and **MLS #**, both at the top of The Property. The social posts still never state a price — that stays deliberate.

## Putting it on the website

Once the website is connected, **Publish to the website** puts the tour or the listing page live and hands you back the link — one button, no files, no emailing anyone.

Fill in **Publishing** (in the panel) once: the publisher address and your key. Ask whoever runs SampleRealtyteam.com for both. Your key is stored in this browser only — treat it like a password, and don't email this file to anyone once it's filled in.

**It won't publish something that isn't ready.** The button stops and tells you why if the address or city is missing, if there are no photos, if the disclaimer line is empty, or if **the fair-housing screen flagged anything** — that one is deliberate: a flagged phrase on a public page is worse than a flagged phrase in a draft. Then it shows you the exact web address and asks before anything goes out.

Until the website is connected the button says **"not connected yet"** and tells you what's missing rather than doing nothing.

## When a listing changes

Every listing has a **Status** at the top of the property fields — Active, Under contract, Sold, Withdrawn. **Change it the day it changes.** It isn't paperwork; it drives what the world sees:

- **Under contract** — the page banner changes from *Just Listed*, and it offers to update the live page
- **Sold or Withdrawn** — it offers to **take the page down**

If a published page disagrees with the listing, a red bar sits above the preview until you fix it, and any *other* listing that's gone out of date is flagged when you open the tool.

**Published pages also expire on their own after 90 days.** Past that they stop showing the listing and show a short "no longer current — please get in touch" note with your name and number instead. Republishing resets the clock. This is deliberate: a page you forget about should go quiet rather than keep telling buyers a house is for sale.

**The honest limit:** all of that runs in your browser. If you never open the tool, nothing is updated or taken down until the 90 days run out. A system that watches the MLS and retires pages without anyone remembering is a real build, and it needs feed access from Canopy.

## The launch checklist

Three groups: what this tool makes now (**Ready**), what needs your judgment or is a compliance step (**You** — MLS publishing is always a person, and so is the day-2 portal check), and what needs the connected build (**System** — hosting, real lead capture, speed-to-lead, feedback). Print it and work down it.

**On checking the portals:** no tool may scrape Zillow, Realtor.com or Redfin — they prohibit it, and anything built that way breaks the moment they change their site. So the day-2 check is yours, and it's on the checklist with what to compare. Checking your assets against the MLS record automatically is a real build, and it needs feed access from Canopy.

## Printing

For appointments and open houses: **100 lb gloss or silk text**, borderless, at actual size. The dark pages are toner-heavy, so decent stock makes a real difference. For the 7-page presentation, sheet protectors in a black binder look better than hole-punching the pages.
