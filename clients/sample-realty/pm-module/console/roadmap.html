  • 1010 Bexton ends July at −$1,830.51. April's $4,331 in repairs exceeded its rents — so those bills were covered by other owners' funds sitting in the same trust account, or a top-up went unrecorded. Invisible in hand-kept books; instant when the ledgers generate themselves.

Why not just buy software

Because the software would still leave you doing the work.

Buildium, DoorLoop and AppFolio are good at tenant portals, online rent payment and screening. At eight doors they're $60–200 a month and weeks of setup — and at the end of it the trust accounting is still yours to type. They're a filing cabinet, not an employee.

The thing that actually costs you time and carries licence risk is the part none of them do for you. So: build the operated piece, and buy the commodity edges only when you need them — tenant screening per applicant, online payment if your tenants ever outgrow Zelle.

The build

What arrives when.

Each phase is useful on its own. Nothing later is a prerequisite for something earlier working.
TodayDone
  • The books engine — journal in, per-property ledgers and NCREC trial balance out, by formula
  • Owner statements drafted for all five owners
  • The findings review you're holding
First monthPhase 2
  • Rent roll and lease registry — one place that knows every property, owner, tenant, rent, due day, lease dates and deposit. One sit-down to confirm what the journal couldn't tell us.
  • Bank statement in — transactions matched to tenant and property automatically; you approve the exceptions only.
  • Rent watchdog — on the 6th, anyone unpaid is flagged and a reminder is drafted. You approve every send.
  • Your console, live — the morning view, updating itself.
After thatPhase 3
  • Maintenance intake — a tenant texts a photo, a ticket drafts itself, you approve the dispatch, and the cost posts to the right property ledger and the owner's statement without anyone typing it twice
  • An owner page per landlord — Raju sees Raju's numbers, whenever he wants, instead of emailing you
  • Compliance calendar — monthly reconciliation, the 30-day deposit clock on any move-out, lease renewals at 90/60/30 days, 1099 totals accumulating all year
  • Vacancies flow to the website — the For Lease section fills itself
As it earns itPhase 4
  • A live bank feed instead of statement uploads
  • Tenant screening when you next need it
  • The routine steps stop asking permission — but only after a clean run of you approving them and agreeing every time

What it will never do

  • It never moves money. It drafts; you execute every disbursement. That doesn't change at any phase.
  • Every figure traces back to a source transaction, with the audit trail kept.
  • Your trust-account data stays isolated. Nothing is shared and nothing is trained on.
  • Counsel reviews the NCREC record-keeping fit before any of it goes live.

The honest summary

What this is, and what it isn't.

What's real today

The books engine ran on your actual data and the findings are traceable to individual journal rows. The console is seeded from those same numbers. You can check any figure against your own workbook.

What isn't built

Everything from the first month onward. The console is a demo of the morning view, not a live system — it isn't reading your bank, and no watchdog is running. That starts when the work does.
