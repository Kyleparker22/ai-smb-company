# Sample Client demo — narrated click-path (~2.5 min)

> For the Founder to walk Client Owner through the mockups. Open the `yourco-sample-client` preview at `index.html`. Resize to mobile (or just hold up a phone-shaped window) for the two approval screens and the greenlit. Read the **SAY** lines near-verbatim; the **→** lines are what to click.

---

**Open on `index.html` (the hub).**

> **SAY:** "Let me show you the whole thing on a sample job — a $48,500 patio. None of it's live, it's all fake data, but it's exactly what yours does. Top to bottom."

---

**→ Point at step 1 (don't click — it's the flow).**

> **SAY:** "The second a proposal hits *Signed* in Aspire — and it only fires on Installation, it ignores your maintenance and mowing — it reads the proposal, applies your deposit tiers, sorts the materials, and spots the subs. It drafts everything. But nothing's gone out yet. That's the point."

---

**→ Click step 2 — "Charlene approves the deposit."**

> **SAY:** "This is Charlene's phone. The deposit's already written, email and text, ready for the client. See the amount — sixteen-nine-seventy-five? That's locked. Computed by code, not the AI, so it's right every time. She can tweak the wording, but she can't fumble the number. She reads it, taps approve…"
>
> **→ Tap "Approve & send."**
>
> **SAY:** "…and *now* it sends. And it logs who approved it and when. Nothing reaches a customer until somebody taps."

---

**→ Back, then click step 3 — "You approve supplier + sub orders."**

> **SAY:** "Same thing on your side, for the orders. Carolina Stone — it already split the delivery: pallets and bulk to the job site, packaged stuff to the shop. Volt Lighting to the shop. And it caught the grading sub and wrote his notice with the dates. You just tap through them."
>
> **→ Tap a couple of "Approve & send."**
>
> **SAY:** "Anything you decline goes back to your team. Nothing sends on its own."

---

**→ Back, then click step 4 — "Watch every job from one board."**

> **SAY:** "This is the part you'll live in. Every signed job, where it stands — deposit, suppliers, subs. The Chen job's waiting on a deposit and one supplier. Whitfield down here is fully greenlit, crew starts the 14th. And up top — it nudges you. Volt Lighting hasn't replied in two days, the Chen deposit's been sitting three. It chases that so you don't have to remember to."

---

**→ Back, then click step 5 — "Greenlit when it's all confirmed."**

> **SAY:** "When a job has everything — deposit in, suppliers locked, sub confirmed — you and Charlene both get this. All systems go, here's your start date. You're not wondering if a job's ready. You know."

---

**→ Back, then click step 6 — "A monthly report of what it did."**

> **SAY:** "Once a month, what it actually did. Proposals handled, deposits out on time, hours saved. And the bottom part matters most: zero messages sent without your approval, every dollar computed by code, never missed a signed proposal. That's what you're really paying for — not the drafting, the reliability."

---

**Close.**

> **SAY:** "That's the whole thing. Signed proposal to greenlit, and all you and Charlene do is tap approve on anything that goes to a customer. Three weeks to build, and I run it from there — you never touch a server or an AI account. Want to do it?"

---

### Tips
- Total run time ~2.5 minutes. Don't rush the approve taps — the "nothing sends until a human taps" moment is the whole pitch.
- If he asks "is the money ever wrong?" — that's the locked-amount on screen 2. Point at it.
- If he asks "what if it screws up?" — screen 6, the reliability block (eval suite, 0 unapproved sends, duplicate caught + blocked).
- Everything shown is sample data; say so once up front so nothing feels overstated.
