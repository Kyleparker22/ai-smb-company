2026-08-09 — Name the number, name the output format, forbid the filler

Source: the bad-prompt/good-prompt pairs the Founder brought 2026-08-09; triaged in `decisions/2026-07-05_tool-triage.md` §Addendum 08-09 (batch).
Pattern: The difference between a prompt that produces usable work and one that produces mush is three things, every time. **(1) A number and a filter** — "list 50 HVAC companies with 100+ reviews, flag any whose hours end at 5pm" beats "find businesses that need AI." **(2) A named artifact** — "output as a table: name, reviews, hours, phone" or "3 sentences an owner reads in 15 seconds" beats "analyse this." **(3) Banned tells** — "no jargon, no 'hope this finds you well'" beats "write a professional email." The vague version isn't a *worse* prompt of the same kind; it produces a different *category* of output — a description of the work instead of the work.
Implication: Applies to how agents are prompted **and** to the copy itself. **Reilly** — sourcing/ICP asks carry a count, a threshold, and the output columns. **Bella** — audit findings state the arithmetic and the read-time ("15 seconds"), never "AI can help here"; the quantified-loss line is the deliverable. **Call prep** — ask for *the three questions most likely to surface the expensive problem, the two most likely objections, and the one number to know*, not "help me prepare." **Michelle** — outbound gets a word count, a concrete number about *their* business in line one, a banned-phrase list, and one question to end on. **The one line worth lifting verbatim into the outbound copy rules: no mention of AI until the last line.** That is the same instinct as yourco's own positioning rule — sell the outcome, never the technology — and it survives the honesty bar because it changes emphasis, not truth. Pairs with `brand/writing-rules.md` (which governs voice); this governs structure and specificity.
