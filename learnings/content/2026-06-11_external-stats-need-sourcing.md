2026-06-11 — External content stating statistics needs a cited source or illustrative framing

Source: Kolby's first eval (`loops/eval-review/2026-06-11.md`), failure mode #1 — Katie's missed-call post.
Pattern: Market stats from a WebSearch sweep (e.g. "≈42 calls/month, 3 in 4 unanswered, ~$21k/mo") got rounded into hard factual claims in a customer-facing post, with no attribution. On an external surface, unsourced statistics are a credibility risk — and YourCo's whole brand is "no fabrication."
Implication: Before any external content publishes, every statistic must either (a) carry a citable source, or (b) be reframed as illustrative ("a typical contractor can lose…", "many contractors miss most calls in season") rather than asserted as a precise fact. Intel-grade numbers from Sadie/WebSearch are directional — fine for internal strategy, not for stating as fact externally without a source. When in doubt, soften to illustrative or cut the number.
