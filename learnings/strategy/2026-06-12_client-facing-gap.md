# Learning — the client-facing surface was a gap, and the Founder had to find it

**Date:** 2026-06-12 · **Type:** miss / systemic · **Surfaced by:** the Founder (not the system — that's the point)

## What happened
After a long sprint building the *acquisition* surface (collateral, demos, configurator, ROI calc, org chart, the Instant Employee), the Founder asked: *"where/how will clients see the agents and get them to work, and see what they're doing on their end?"* The answer exposed a hole: the **client-facing console** — where a paying client watches their employee work, approves drafts, sees outcomes and reliability — **did not exist.** Only a weekly readout did. The literal moat (observability + approval + trust) had no daily client-facing surface. Then the Founder asked the sharper question: *why did I have to surface this, not you or the agents?*

## The honest diagnosis — two systemic causes (not bad luck)
1. **An ownership hole in the org design.** Map the roster against the client lifecycle: acquisition is heavily staffed (Sadie, Reilly, Pickle, David), build is staffed (Kimi, Kemba), eval/ops is staffed (Kolby, Atlas, Rafi), expansion is staffed (Bird). But **no agent owned "the client's daily lived experience of the employee."** Kortney did engagement *health* — but as periodic readouts, not as DRI for the client-facing product surface. When no one's mandate covers a thing, no one advocates for it, and it doesn't get built. The gap was organizationally predictable.
2. **The system optimizes the active thread and what already exists — nothing hunts for what's absent.** The closed-loop discipline (observe pattern → learning → adjust) is excellent at improving processes that *run*. But absence produces no bad run to observe — a surface that simply doesn't exist generates no signal. And the assistant, in execution mode all session, followed the conversation's gradient (build the front door) productively without independently stepping back to walk the *whole* lifecycle and ask "what's missing after they buy?" A COO-minded operator asks that by default. Neither the assistant nor any agent was running that loop.

## The real finding
The most important miss wasn't the console. It was that **the OS needed the founder to find a foundational gap.** Building the console fixes the instance. The system fix is installing a loop that finds the *next* absence without the Founder.

## The fix (feed-forward — what changes from now)
- **Assign the owner.** Webb builds/maintains the client console (it's a product surface). **Kortney's mandate expands** from "health readouts" to **DRI for the client-facing experience** — is the client seeing the moat operate, daily? Someone now owns the client's side of the glass.
- **Install the negative-space review** — a recurring gap-audit that walks the full client lifecycle + every company surface and lists what *should* exist but doesn't. Spec: `processes/gap-audit.md`. Owner: Brett (strategy), monthly + at every major build milestone. This is the completeness-critic step the loop was missing.
- **Assistant behavior change:** at the end of a build sprint, before declaring done, zoom out and walk the full lifecycle (acquire → buy → onboard → *use daily* → see value → expand) and name what's unbuilt. Don't only chase the stated next item.

## Read this at Step 0
Brett (gap-audit), Kortney (client experience), Webb (console), and the assistant on any "what should we build next" turn. The test for completeness is not "did the thing we built work" — it's "what part of the client's experience can't they see or do yet?"
