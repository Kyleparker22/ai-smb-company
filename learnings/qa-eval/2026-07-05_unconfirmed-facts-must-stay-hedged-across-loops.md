2026-07-05 — An unconfirmed fact must stay a hedged binary until a primary record lands; watch the Slack condensation, that's where hedges get dropped

Source: Kolby's eval (`loops/eval-review/2026-07-05.md`), tracing the 6/25 Client Owner meeting across a week of loops. No primary record of the meeting exists (no calendar event, no CRM readout, the Founder never logged it). The uncertainty eroded as the fact traveled between loops: the sales *artifact* (06-29) correctly hedged — "either it happened and wasn't logged, or it slipped"; the sales *Slack post* (06-29) dropped the hedge — "the conversation happened but wasn't reconciled"; customer-health (Kortney, 07-01) hardened it further — "did happen" + "Slack confirmed the 6/25 discovery occurred," where the only "Slack confirmation" was the sales post's own inference. Meanwhile Jim's desk (07-03), open-loops (07-05), and the 07-04 full-OS audit all correctly held it as a binary ("no logged record — happened off-calendar OR slipped"). No link was a fabrication; every one was hedged or sourced. It was telephone-drift: an inference cited downstream as confirmation.
Pattern: A fact with no primary record can harden from "maybe" to "confirmed" as it passes between loops, because (a) the Slack condensation of an artifact silently drops the artifact's hedge to read cleaner, and (b) a downstream loop then cites the upstream loop's *inference* as a source ("Slack confirmed it"). Two properties of a claim get conflated: *attributed* (a source is named) and *grounded* (the source is a primary record). Attribution to another loop's guess passes the "cite it" bar but fails the grounding bar.
Implication: Two checkable verbs. (1) When a loop summarizes to Slack, the summary must preserve the artifact's uncertainty — if the artifact says "A or B," the Slack line may not say "A." Before posting, ask: "does this summary assert a branch the artifact left open?" (2) When grading or writing (Kolby, any loop citing another loop): a cited *source* only grounds a claim if it's a primary record (calendar event, CRM field, a human's own statement), not another loop's inference about the same fact. An unconfirmed fact stays a hedged binary until a primary record lands. Extends the 06-21 checklist-form and 06-28 verify-when-load-bearing learnings — this is the checkable verb for cross-loop facts: *preserve-the-hedge*.
