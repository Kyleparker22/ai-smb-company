2026-08-13 — A trip-wire whose check is already true on the day you write it is not a trip-wire

Source: the Founder's agent-substrate build session (`decisions/2026-08-13_agent-substrate-upgrade.md`). Caught by running `dashboard/tripwires.py` immediately after writing the decision, not by review.
Pattern: The decision's trip-wire read `Overturn if: 30+ days of run-journal data show the substrate is not being used`, with `Check: loopsStale >= 14`. `loopsStale` was **already 14 that morning**, so HQ reported the decision as `contradicted` within an hour of it being written — before the 30 days of data its own prose depends on could possibly exist. The prose and the check were describing different things: the prose described a *future* observation, the check described a *current* state that happened to correlate with it. This is a quiet failure, because `contradicted` is the loudest verdict the engine produces and it was firing on a decision nobody had had a chance to be wrong about.
Implication: When writing a trip-wire, run `python3 dashboard/tripwires.py` **before committing** and confirm the new decision reads `watching`, not `contradicted`. If the check could plausibly be true today, fence it with a `daysSinceDecision >= N` term (the grammar supports it, all-`and` joined) where N is the time the prose actually requires. Rule of thumb: **if the Overturn-if sentence contains a duration ("30 days of", "after a quarter of", "once we have run"), the check needs a `daysSinceDecision` term or it is measuring something else.** Related: a check that covers only part of the prose still needs its `Check covers` line — the time fence does not substitute for that.
