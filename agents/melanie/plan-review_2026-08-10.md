# Business plan review — Melanie, 2026-08-10

**Scope:** `06_business-plan.md` v1.2, checked against `finance/yourco-financial-model.xlsx` as it stands after Charles's review, the four cleanups, and the concurrent Cash & Runway fix. My standing duty is the prose, not the figures — Section 8's numbers are Charles's. My job is whether the document still tells the truth about them.

**Verdict: the numbers are clean, the story has one hole.** Every figure I checked in §8 matches the model exactly — thirteen assertions, all present and correct, and they survived two subsequent changes to the workbook. The realignment held. What has not kept up is **Capital**: the model now answers a question §11 still describes as open, and the answer is forty times larger than the number on the page.

I propose five changes. the Founder approves; I have made none of them.

---

## 1 · §11 Capital understates what the founders actually put in — and no longer needs to

The plan says peak drawdown is "under $1,600 in every case", then adds, honestly, that the figure "charges nothing for principal time before the salary trigger… so the true hole is larger."

That caveat has been overtaken. The model now computes it:

| | |
|---|---|
| Peak cash drawdown | **$1,528** |
| Unpaid principal time (15 principal-months before the first draw at month 6) | **$62,500** |
| **Total investment to profitability** | **$64,028** |

$1,528 and $64,028 are different answers to "what does this cost you to start." The plan currently leads with the first and gestures at the second. For a document whose named readers are *"a partner, that partner's counsel, or a bank"*, the total is the number they came for — and it is still a small number, which is the argument. Leading with $1,528 reads as either naïve or evasive depending on the reader's mood, and neither is what we want from a section titled Capital.

**Propose:** state both, with the total leading. The honest sentence is that the cash requirement is trivial and the real investment is five months of three people working unpaid.

## 2 · The contribution asymmetry is unnamed, and it is the first thing a partner's adviser will find

The model now splits the cash 50 / 35 / 15 — **$764 / $535 / $229**. But the unpaid time is contributed **equally**: five months each, three principals, fifteen principal-months.

So Mike puts in the same five months of unpaid work as the Founder, funds $229 against the Founder's $764, and owns 15% against the Founder's 50%.

That is very probably correct and intended — the Founder contributes the entire pre-existing business through Schedule B, and the Distribution Threshold in §6.1A exists precisely to protect that pre-grant value. **But the plan never says it.** Someone reading this document on Mike's behalf will construct that comparison within a page of §7, and it is far better that we drew it ourselves, with the Schedule B answer attached, than that they draw it and wonder why we didn't.

**Propose:** one paragraph in §7 stating the asymmetry and the reason for it.

## 3 · The Year-0 milestones omit the only thing on the calendar

§9's Year-0 block exists to define "progress no gate can block." It lists the warm engagement, the collaboration paper, counsel, in-person channels, the leading indicator, and the 48h validation. All still correct.

It does not mention the **Partner B lock-in schedule — 8/11 to 8/26, roughly twelve hours across six sessions**, walking every domain of the company to a locked baseline. That starts **tomorrow**. It is the most concrete, most dated, most certain activity in the company right now, it is squarely gate-independent, and it is the mechanism by which most of the other Year-0 items either move or are shown not to be moving.

A Year-0 section about un-blockable progress that omits the one scheduled block of un-blockable progress is not describing the company.

**Propose:** add it, with its dates.

## 4 · The risk ranking no longer matches the risks

§10 ranks **#1 the launch gate never defines itself** and **#4 the partner admission is mis-structured**. That ordering was right this morning. Today:

- the partnership went from one partner to two, at 50 / 35 / 15
- the Founder holds exactly half, which is not control, and the two partners together also hold exactly half
- the deadlock end-game was deleted with nothing yet in its place
- **counsel gate #14 regressed from counsel-ready to hard-blocked** — it had been the closest gate to clearing
- three governance decisions (D10, D11, D12) are open and each blocks signing
- one partner's contribution is unrecorded anywhere in the company's files

Meanwhile #1 is exactly where it was. Nine gates are red.

Risk #4 got materially worse today and #1 did not change. I am not certain #4 should displace #1 — the gate still blocks all external revenue, which is a bigger category of harm. But the current ordering was set when the partnership was two people and settled, and it is now stale.

**Propose:** #1 and #4 as joint-first, with #4's text carrying today's specifics.
