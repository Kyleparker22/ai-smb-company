## 48 hours, documented.

Most AI projects measure time-to-live in quarters. We measure it in hours. Here's a build — the actual clock — for the first capability of your AI OS .
Example build: an intake agent — answers every call and text, qualifies the inquiry, books the next step. It’s the first piece of the OS to go live; the rest follow, one at a time. The clock starts when the agreement is signed and access is in.
- Onboarding opens the engagement and provisions the agent's identity — its own email in the client's tenant. (Tenant access = client-approved.)
- A four-question pre-call intake goes out so discovery starts from facts.
- The job: answer every inbound, qualify it, book the estimate, confirm, log — day or night.
- Locked the qualification fields, the calendar rules, the brand voice, and the approval line.
- Picked the stack for the shape: text/SMS + Calendar + CRM log.
- Overlaid the client's logic on a proven blueprint — the agent's actual reasoning, written.
- Wired the live connectors: book to the calendar, send the confirmation, log the lead.
- Set the approval gates (customer-facing actions wait for sign-off) and turned on cost tracking.
- Ran the test set end-to-end — every downstream action fired (booking, confirmation, log).
- Red-teamed it: refused to fabricate a price, escalated an out-of-area lead, held against a prompt injection. 0 breaches.
- Brand voice approved by the client. Watchdogs + human-fallback wired.
- Pointed the client's intake at the agent. Soft launch — watched the first real texts closely.
- Sent the go-live note: what's live, how to reach a human, what to expect.
- 48 hours from signed → the first capability of your AI OS, live in the business. Weekly improvement starts now — and the next capability follows when you’re ready.
