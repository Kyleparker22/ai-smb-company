The reliability layer, explained.

The part you can't see is the part you're buying. Anyone can wire up an agent now — making a whole AI OS safe to run inside your business is the real work. Here's what that actually means, in plain English.

🎯

Grounding

It answers only from your real business — your hours, prices, availability, policies — not from "general internet knowledge."
Like a new hire trained on your binder, not someone improvising at the front desk.
no made-up answers to your customers.
✅

Eval gates

Before it goes live — and every week after — it's scored against a fixed rubric: does it qualify right, book right, escalate right, stay on-brand?
A driving test it has to keep passing, not a one-time demo that looked good once.
"it passed" means it actually works, reliably.
🐶

Watchdogs

Automated monitors that catch failures and silent drift — a booking that didn't fire, a dip in quality.
A manager doing spot-checks, around the clock, who never gets tired.
problems get caught before you — or your customer — notice.
🚦

Approval boundary

It can draft, prepare, and handle the safe stuff — but it can't send, delete, or spend without a human until it's earned that trust.
A sharp new employee who runs the big calls past you first.
"always-on" never means "unsupervised on day one."
🛡️

Red-teaming

Before launch, we actively try to break it — prompt injection, abuse, attempts to grab data or fabricate answers.
Crash-testing a car before it ships, not after.
it's proven to hold under pressure, not just on a good day. Try it yourself →
🤝

Accountability

Someone owns keeping it good. It isn't a tool you're handed and left to operate.
The difference between buying a lawnmower and hiring a landscaper.
when something's off, it's our problem to fix — not your new side-job.
