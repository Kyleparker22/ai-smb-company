# Content franchise — "What my AI employees did this week"

> **Owner: Katie.** A *repeatable* content series, not one-off posts — the glass box (`agents/webb/pages/yourco-site-v2/glass-box.html`) turned into an ongoing engine. Each issue shares a few real things yourco's own digital employees actually did that week. **Only yourco can credibly run this** — it requires being a company actually run by AI employees. That's the moat, as content.

## Why it works
- **Proof, not claims.** Pre-revenue, we have no client logos — but we have a pile of real work products. This series leads with them.
- **Un-copyable.** A competitor can't run "what my AI employees did this week" unless they actually run on AI employees. We do.
- **Compounds.** A weekly drumbeat that builds a body of evidence + a recognizable franchise, vs. scattered posts.

## Format
- **Cadence:** weekly (folds into Katie's Friday content loop) — LinkedIn + the newsletter.
- **Shape:** a short intro line → **3–4 real moments** (one or two sentences each) → one takeaway that ladders to the moat ("this is what reliability looks like — and it's what we'd run for you").
- **Voice:** plain, specific, a little wry; no hype, no buzzwords (per `brand/v0/brand-guidelines.md`). Lead with the most *surprising or relatable* moment.

## Hard rules (the credibility gate)
- **Real only.** Pull from actual artifacts in `loops/` — never invent a moment, a number, or an outcome (`learnings/content/2026-06-11_external-stats-need-sourcing.md`).
- **Redact** anything sensitive (no client data, no PII, no exact internal figures we wouldn't share).
- **Show the unglamorous + the honest** — an employee catching its *own* mistake is the best material; it proves the reliability layer is real.
- Kolby's eval applies before publish; the Founder approves the send.
