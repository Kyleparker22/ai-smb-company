# Reed Demo Script — Atlas: The Monday Briefing

**Production ID:** 2026-06-07_atlas-monday-briefing
**Subject:** Atlas (YourCo's ops digital employee) producing the Monday Morning Briefing
**Use case dramatized:** Founder/exec ops — "your Monday starts with a 5-minute read that tells you exactly what to do this week, without opening another tool."
**Vertical fit:** Generic/founder + any exec-ops buyer. First reusable demo; also the proof-of-concept for the production pipeline.
**Target length:** 75–90 seconds.
**Status:** draft script — awaiting the Founder approval before build/publish.

---

## Why this is the first demo
Atlas actually ran this morning (2026-06-07) and produced a real briefing — including catching a real watchdog (the Google Workspace card decline that both the sales and finance loops flagged). So the **credibility gate is trivially satisfied**: every frame shows real, running behavior. Nothing is mocked. This is exactly the "show, don't tell" asset YourCo's moat depends on.

## Format & credibility rules
- **Core = screen capture of the real workspace** (the loops, the briefing artifact, the Slack post, the Gmail draft). No talking-head, no stock footage.
- Light intro/outro card + captions + voiceover. Optional dynamic intro line per vertical in a later version.
- **Delivery:** hosted landing page + animated thumbnail/GIF + tracked link. **Never** embed/attach the video in email (deliverability rule shared with Reilly).
- Voiceover: calm, direct, founder-to-founder. No hype, no buzzwords. Matches the Founder's voice.

## Storyboard + voiceover

### Scene 1 — The problem (0:00–0:12)
**On screen:** Cold open on a cluttered Monday — a calendar, an inbox at "99+ unread," a couple of dashboards. Title card fades in: *"Monday. Where do you even start?"*
**VO:** "Monday morning. Pipeline's in one tool, money's in another, your inbox is mostly noise. Before you've done anything useful, an hour's gone."
**Caption:** Monday morning, scattered across five tools.

### Scene 2 — Meet Atlas (0:12–0:24)
**On screen:** Cut to the clean YourCo workspace. Cursor opens the `processes/loops/` folder showing the sales, finance, and monday-briefing SOPs. A simple lower-third: *"Atlas — a digital employee. Runs 7:30am, every Monday."*
**VO:** "This is Atlas. Not a chatbot, not a dashboard — a digital employee with a standing job: read everything, every Monday, and tell you what matters."
**Caption:** Atlas — a named digital employee, not another dashboard.

### Scene 3 — It does the work (0:24–0:45)
**On screen:** Screen capture, sped up: Atlas reads the sales loop artifact, then the finance loop artifact. Highlight the moment both independently flag the **Google Workspace card decline**. Show the cross-cutting watchdog being raised to the top of the briefing.
**VO:** "It reads the sales pulse and the finance pulse on its own. This week, both flagged the same thing — a declined card on the account that runs every company mailbox. Two systems agreeing. Atlas caught it and led with it."
**Caption:** Cross-checks every source. Catches what you'd miss.

### Scene 4 — The output (0:45–1:05)
**On screen:** The finished `loops/monday-briefing/2026-06-07.md` scrolls — What changed, Pipeline, Finance pulse (watchdog at top), Recommended actions. Then a quick three-up: the **Markdown artifact**, the **Gmail draft** in the inbox, the **Slack post** in #all-yourco — all the same briefing, delivered three ways.
**VO:** "Five minutes later, the briefing's done — under 800 words, the watchdog up top, five prioritized moves for the week. Delivered three ways: a file you can keep, a draft in your inbox, a note in Slack. You just read it."
**Caption:** One 5-minute read. Delivered where you already work.

### Scene 5 — The YourCo difference (1:05–1:20)
**On screen:** Subtle overlay on the artifact highlighting the eval/watchdog/approval markers (the "What I'd do differently next run" feedback line; the watchdog flag; the ≤800-word check). Lower-third: *"Reliability · Eval · Watchdogs · Approval."*
**VO:** "And it's built the way the boring-but-critical parts demand — evals, watchdogs, approval gates, a feedback loop that makes it better each week. That's the part you can't vibe-code. That's the part we own."
**Caption:** The reliability layer is the product.

### Scene 6 — Close / CTA (1:20–1:30)
