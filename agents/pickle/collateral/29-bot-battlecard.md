# Battlecard — yourco vs. the $29 AI receptionist

> **Owner: Pickle.** The objection that *will* come up: *"Why wouldn't I just use one of those $29/mo AI receptionist apps?"* Sadie validated the demand for the intake employee **and** the crowded $29–59/mo floor that owns the cheap end (`learnings/sales-copy/2026-06-11_intake-competitive-floor.md`). This is how we answer — honestly, no commission breath, no trashing competitors. Use on calls + in Pickle collateral; the *spirit* (managed employee, not a tool) threads into Reilly's copy without the price war.

## The one-liner
**A $29 bot is a *tool you run yourself*. yourco is a *managed employee that runs for you*.** Different things — and the difference is exactly the part that was costing you money.

## The honest frame: buy a tool vs. hire an employee
- The $29 app = **buy a tool.** You set it up, you script it, you babysit it, and when it mishandles a lead at 8pm on a Saturday, **that's on you.**
- yourco = **hire an employee.** We design it, deploy it in *your* tenant, run it, eval it, and improve it weekly. **You own the outcome — never the tokens, models, or infrastructure.**

## Side by side
| | $29 AI receptionist | yourco digital employee |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | a self-serve **tool** you configure | a **managed employee** we run for you |
| Setup + upkeep | **yours** — you build + maintain it | **ours** — live in 48h, improved every week |
| Capability | one trick (answer / take a message) | qualify → book → confirm → log → follow up → **escalate** |
| When it's wrong | no one — it's your tool | **someone's accountable** (that's the whole model) |
| Reliability | hope it works | **held to eval, watchdog, and approval standards** |
| Where it lives | a third-party app | **in your tenant**, its own email, like a real hire |
| Improves? | only if you tinker | **weekly**, against real performance |

## The price reframe (don't fight on $29)
The $29 isn't the cost — **your time setting it up, plus the leads it still drops, plus no one accountable** is the cost. yourco is priced to the **managed-employee outcome** — it replaces what a part-time hire would do, without the part-time hire. We don't compete with the $29 tool on price; we compete with the *missed-revenue problem* the tool was supposed to solve and usually doesn't.

## When a $29 bot is genuinely the right call (be honest)
If they want a cheap voicemail-replacement they're happy to run themselves, and they're fine being the one accountable when it fumbles — a $29 app is fine, and we'll say so. **yourco is for the owner who wants the *outcome* handled — reliably, in their tenant, without managing it.** Saying this out loud builds the trust that closes the ones who *do* want the employee.

## Proof to show, not tell
- **The demo gallery** (`see it work →`) — watch an employee qualify, book, *and* know its limits (escalate clinical/legal, refuse to fabricate). A $29 bot can't show that judgment.
- **The reliability moat** — eval gates, watchdogs, the approval gate, a red-team that tries to break it. That rigor *is* the product, and it's invisible in a $29 app until it fails.

## What NOT to do
- Don't name or trash specific competitors. Draw the distinction; let them conclude.
- Don't lead with this — let *them* raise the $29 question, then reframe to buy-a-tool vs. hire-an-employee.
- Don't discount toward the floor. If they only want $29, they're not the ICP — and that's fine.
