# YourCo — Sales Battlecard

> For the Founder on calls. Internal. Plain answers, no scripts to read verbatim. Keep honest — pre-revenue; don't overclaim.

## The one-liner

**When they ask what an AI OS is** (the most-repeated question on every call — answer it with the formula, not a definition):

> "Your answering service + your CRM + your email marketing + your scheduler + your bookkeeper + your SOP binder. Except they talk to each other, and they do the work. That's your AI OS."

Then close on what they can't buy anywhere else: *"You own the outcome; we own everything that makes it reliable."*

**The formula sells the WHAT. It cannot sell the WHY US.** Reliability, evals, approvals, the model-upgrade dividend — none of those are visible in a sum of familiar parts, and a no-code operator could recite the same formula honestly. So the formula opens and the moat closes. If the formula is doing the moat's job, we've argued ourselves down into the commodity layer we compete against.

**The on-ramp line** (offered last, per lead-high-land-anywhere — never lead with this): "If you'd rather start with one piece, we'll build a single named employee, live in 48 hours, and expand from there."

## vs the alternatives
- **vs DIY / no-code (ChatGPT, Zapier, an agent builder):** Building an agent is the easy 20%. Making it reliable enough to run unsupervised — evals, watchdogs, approvals, observability, security — is the 80%, and it's a job, not a weekend. We own that layer; you don't absorb the risk.
- **vs an agency / consultant:** No 6-month, six-figure project. Fixed fee, live in 48 hours, and we *operate* it ongoing — not a deck and a handoff.
- **vs a SaaS co-pilot (per-seat tool):** A co-pilot is software you still have to learn, manage, and make useful. This is an employee that does the job. You manage an outcome, not a tool.
- **vs hiring a person:** No recruiting, ramp, turnover, or benefits. Live in days, improves every week, and the marginal cost of the next role is a fraction of a headcount.

## Objection handling
- **"How do I know it won't mess up with my customers?"** Anything customer-facing is drafted for your approval until you've seen enough to trust it. We earn autonomy in stages — it prepares and drafts first, acts unsupervised only once proven.
- **"Is this just ChatGPT with a wrapper?"** The model is a commodity. What you're buying is the reliability layer — grounding in your business, eval gates, watchdogs, approval flows, and someone accountable for keeping it good.
- **"What about my data / security?"** It runs in your tenant, touches only what it needs, and we apply commercially reasonable security. We never sell your data. (Data terms in the engagement agreement.)
- **"What does it really cost?"** A fixed build fee + a flat monthly retainer that covers the employee *and* all infrastructure — you never see a token or model bill. Exact numbers are scoped to the role on the call.
- **"What if I want out / what do I keep?"** Month-to-month after the initial term. You keep your data and the work product the employee produced for you.
- **"Why you — you're new?"** We run our own company on the same digital employees we'd build for you, held to the same standards. We don't ship anything we haven't trusted ourselves first.

## Good-fit signals (qualify)
- Owner/leader-led; time is the constraint, not strategy.
- A clear, repetitive, high-volume job (intake, follow-up, scheduling, drafting, back-office).
- Has the tools the employee needs (calendar, CRM/phone) or is willing to use a simple stand-in.
- Wants an outcome owned for them — not a tool to run themselves.

## Not-a-fit signals (disqualify honestly)
- Wants a SaaS product to operate themselves → point to a co-pilot vendor.
- Systems need ground-up rebuilding before anything can sit on top → tell them, refer out.
- Expecting fully-autonomous, zero-oversight from day one → reset to staged trust.

## FAQ
- **Really 48 hours?** From a signed agreement + the access we need, the first use case is live in 48h. Improvement is ongoing after.
- **One employee or many?** Start with the one role eating the most time; add others once it's trusted.
- **Who maintains it?** We do — evals, watchdogs, weekly iteration. That's the retainer.
