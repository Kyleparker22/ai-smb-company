Change-one-sweep-all was not run. ## 1 · Internal coherence

**1a. §1 vs §4 — "vertically-specific" survived the horizontal edit.** The exec summary's first sentence sells "vertically-specific AI agents, Operating Systems, and Platforms"; §4 declares positioning *and* targeting horizontal the same day. If "vertically-specific" means "fit to that business," say "custom" or "business-specific." *Edit: §1, first paragraph, one word.*

**1b. §4 goes further than canon, and canon wasn't swept.** The 2026-06-22 decision was horizontal *positioning*, concentrated *targeting* (CLAUDE.md still carries the "GTM nuance" paragraph saying exactly that; `01_company.md` §ICP still names landscaping as the locked lead vertical). Today's §4 supersedes both without updating either. Either the decision changed — then log it (`log-decision`) and sweep CLAUDE.md + `01_company.md` + the outbound docs in the same commit — or §4 is overreaching canon. *Edit: log the decision, run the sweep. This is the #1 cross-session failure mode by our own rules.*

**1c. The honest answer on referral density.** Does dropping the beachhead weaken the referral-density logic? **Partially yes — but not where it first appears.** A warm network is socially clustered even when it isn't industry-clustered, so *referral flow* survives warm-first reasonably well. What weakens is **proof transferability and asset reuse**: "they built this for a hardscaper like me" closes; "they built something for someone" doesn't. The demo kits, campaign kits, and case studies are all built to compound within an industry. §4's "depth emerges from where referrals compound" converts a *chosen* concentration into a *hoped-for* one. That's defensible pre-launch (the warm network is what exists), but the plan should state the mechanism, not the hope: after each close, deliberately mine that client's industry for the next 2–3 intros. Emergence with a hand on the wheel. *Edit: §4/§5, one sentence each.*

**1d. §3 pricing frame vs the per-vertical lock rule.** §3 gives horizontal OS bands ($3–10k/mo, Polo prices) while also anchoring on "legacy tier anchors… landscaping intake, locked." `01_company.md` still carries the rule "every new vertical gets its own pricing decision doc locked before the first outbound campaign — no prospecting at unlocked prices." Under all-industries targeting, most warm intros land in *unlocked* verticals. Which regime governs a warm deal in an unlocked industry — the horizontal OS bands, or the vertical-lock prerequisite? The plan is silent and the two rules collide. *Flag to Polo: decide whether the OS bands are now the horizontal default (killing the per-vertical prerequisite for warm/OS deals) and write it down. Edit: §3, one clause.*

**1e. §1/§14 vs §8 — the exec summary sells the OS; the model prices the down-sell.** §1 leads with the OS as "the product and primary revenue generator" at $3–10k/mo; §8 models a $850 blended retainer — the single-module entry price. The floor-flag caveat is honest and I'd keep it, but the headline "0 → $240k → $2M → $7M" numbers in §1 are the *floor* numbers presented as *the path*. A sharp reader concludes either the OS motion isn't expected to convert, or the headline is sandbagged. Needs Polo's OS-mix scenario (finding 4 below) to resolve.

## 2 · Assumptions most likely to be wrong

**2a. Retention 80–85% (annual logo), asserted pre-revenue.** "Sticky once embedded" is a hypothesis with n=0 — the warmest possible prospect (Sample Client, brotherhood pricing, $0 kickoff) has not signed. Year-1 SMB churn on a novel operated service from an unproven founder will run worse than 15–20% before stickiness is demonstrated. *Not asking for a new number (Polo) — asking §8 to mark this the single most sensitive unvalidated assumption and show what 65% does to the Y3 line.*

**2b. The blended-retainer path ($850→$1,300) and the OS-first motion can't both be the plan.** See 1e. Either the mix shifts to OS-tier (blended goes far above $1,300, client counts drop) or it doesn't (and the OS-first strategy underperformed). §8 currently models a world where the strategy in §1–3 mostly fails and we sell entry modules forever. That's a fine *floor*; it shouldn't be the *only* scenario.

**2c. ~24 clients EOY1 vs one founder + part-time contractor + the white-glove rule.** The pre-mortem's own mitigation (#3) says first 3–5 deliveries are white-glove, the Founder-in-the-loop, explicitly not the autonomous model. 24 clients in year one requires the autonomy bet to be *proven* by roughly client 5 and delivery to be genuinely agent-carried thereafter — the central unproven bet (#5), now with a partner whose lane is undefined and therefore contributes zero modeled capacity. Also note Year 1 starts "at launch," a date that does not exist: the entire timeline floats on an undefined gate.

**2d. Gross margin 80%+ / $100–300 absorbed cost per client.** Pre-mortem #4 (unit-economics inversion on heavy voice/volume clients) is unretired and there is no real per-client cost data yet. Fine as stated assumption; §8's pressure-test note should name per-client `cost.md` actuals as the first replacement input.
