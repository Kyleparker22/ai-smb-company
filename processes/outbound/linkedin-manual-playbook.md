# LinkedIn — the manual playbook (founder-led social selling)

> LinkedIn must stay **manual** (no scraping/automation — `agents/rafi/social-platform-scraping-assessment.md`; it's the highest-enforcement platform). So don't try to make it a bulk outbound channel — that's where people get banned and burn the brand. Use it for what it's actually good at: **founder-led, relationship-first social selling.** Low volume, high trust. Owners: **the Founder** (the face + the sends) · **Katie** (content) · **Michelle/Sadie** (draft, never send) · **David** (log warm ones in the CRM once there's contact).

## The mindset
LinkedIn is a **warm/relationship channel, not a cold-blast channel.** One thoughtful, personalized touch beats 100 automated connection requests (which also get your account restricted). The agents *assist* (find, draft, research); **the Founder does the human actions** (connect, comment, DM, post). It's the opposite of the Instantly motion — and that's fine, because the goal here is trust, not volume.

## The five moves (in order of leverage)
1. **Profile as a landing page.** the Founder's headline + About = "I build AI employees that run real work for [trades/SMBs] — live in 48 hours." Featured section links the demo + the glass box. This converts every profile view (people *will* look before replying).
2. **Founder content (the engine).** LinkedIn is one of **Katie's** channels (`processes/content/channel-roadmap.md`): the glass-box / build-in-public posts, the Founder's voice, ~2–3x/week. **This does most of the work** — it makes the cold DM warm because they've already seen you. Katie scripts → Reed produces any visual → **the Founder posts**.
3. **Engage before you reach out.** Comment genuinely on target prospects' + industry posts for a week before connecting. Warm the relationship first; you're a familiar name by the time you connect. (Sadie can surface *which* posts/people; the Founder comments.)
4. **Personalized connection requests (low volume).** Use **Sales Navigator** (manual, paid seat) to find ICP owners. Send **personalized** requests — reference their business/post, **no pitch in the request**. Cap it (≈10–20/day, human-paced) — high accept rate matters more than volume, and it keeps the account safe.
5. **Value-first DM after they connect.** Not a pitch — a useful thing: "saw you're juggling X — here's a 60-sec demo of how we'd handle it for [their company]." **Michelle drafts** the message (intent/brand-aware); **the Founder sends.** One helpful touch, then let the content keep nurturing.

## Where the agents help (and the hard line)
- **Sadie** — finds the people/posts worth engaging (intent signals), surfaces them.
- **Michelle** — drafts the connection note + the value-first DM (applies `brand/writing-rules.md`).
- **Katie** — the founder content cadence.
- **the Founder** — *every* connect, comment, DM, and post is a human action. **No automation tools, no bulk senders** (they violate ToS + risk the account). Drafts in, human out — the same fast-approve discipline as the rest of Sadie's engagement.
- **David** — once a LinkedIn conversation yields contact info / real interest, *then* it becomes a CRM lead (the contact-info gate). Until then it's a relationship in progress, not a CRM row.

## What NOT to do
- ❌ Automated connection requests / auto-DM tools (Dux-Soup, etc.) — ToS violation, account bans.
- ❌ Scraping profiles for emails — covered by the no-scrape posture; use licensed B2B data if you need contact data at volume.
- ❌ Treating it like Instantly — LinkedIn is dozens of quality touches a week, not thousands.

## The honest take
LinkedIn won't be a volume engine for yourco — and that's okay. Its job is **warm credibility**: the founder content makes you known, the engagement makes you familiar, and the manual DM converts the ones already half-sold by the time you message them. Pair it with the *real* volume engines (Instantly cold email + the intent pipeline) rather than forcing it to be one.
