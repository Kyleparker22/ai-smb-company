Part one

The thesis

AI intelligence is becoming a commodity. The models get better, cheaper, and more available to everyone every few months, and nobody who simply uses a model gains an advantage from that.

What is not commoditizing is deployment — getting AI to do real work inside a real business, reliably, in a way an owner will actually trust with their customers and their money.

That's the gap. Most small and mid-size businesses now know AI could run large parts of their operation. Almost none of them can get from knowing that to having it running. They don't have the time, the technical staff, or any way to judge whether what they built is safe to leave alone.

yourco closes that gap as a service, not as software.

Part two

The moat

Anyone can wire up a chatbot. The tooling is free and getting easier every month. So the obvious question is what stops a client — or a competitor — from just doing it themselves.

The answer is everything that happens after the thing works once.

Reliability
It works on the 500th job, not just the demo. Requires building for failure, not for the pitch.
Evaluation
Measured proof that it's right, not a feeling. You have to build the scoring before you can trust the system.
Observability
You can see what it did, and why. Logging, audit trails, replay.
Approval gates
Nothing consequential happens without a human until it has earned the right. This is the trust unlock — and it's a discipline, not a feature.
Integration
It plugs into the systems the business already runs on. Every business is different. This is real work.
Executive trust
The owner sleeps at night. This is the actual product.

A no-code operator can build the first version. They cannot build this. That layer is where the margin lives, and it's why the offering is a service yourco operates rather than software the client is handed.

One consequence worth understanding, because it's the standing answer to "won't this be obsolete in a year?" — because yourco owns the stack and sells the outcome, every model improvement flows to the client as a free upgrade. The offering appreciates as AI advances. Any tool the client could have bought instead depreciates.

Part three

What yourco actually sells

The motion is: an audit first, then a custom AI OS.

The audit

Every engagement starts with a paid diagnostic. yourco goes through the business, finds where the time and money are leaking, and quantifies it. The client gets a real report whether or not they buy anything else. If they do buy, the audit fee is credited in full against the build.

This exists because it solves the two hardest problems in this business at once: it gets paid for discovery instead of giving it away, and it means nobody is ever sold a system before somebody understood their business.

The AI OS

Then yourco builds and operates a custom multi-agent system fit to that business. Not a tool the client has to learn — a system that does work, and that yourco owns the reliability of.

Scope is mapped across eight areas of a business:

Intake·Sales·Marketing·Customer·Operations·Back Office·Company Brain·Training

Each piece can ship in one of three shapes: a named "digital employee" with its own email inside the client's system, a headless automation nobody ever sees, or an AI feature embedded in the client's own product for their customers to use.

There are four tiers, by how much of the business it covers — Core, Suite, Operation, Command. Pricing is drafted but not locked; we'll go through the real numbers in Session 5 rather than hand-wave them here.

The client never touches tokens, models, or infrastructure. yourco absorbs all of it. The client gets an outcome.

Who it's for: small and mid-size businesses — large enough to feel the pain of manual operations, too small to hire an AI team. Not restricted by industry.

Eighty-four new code files against twelve commercial touches. That ratio is the whole story.

  • The always-on system is currently switched off. It was paused on August 4th. Nobody noticed until an audit found it on the 9th — including the monitor whose entire job is catching exactly that.
  • The infrastructure is failing for non-payment. The server the whole system runs on has had its bill decline twice and is still unpaid. The AI service went dark on July 30th when credits ran out.
  • No lawyer has ever been engaged. Twelve separate things are blocked waiting on legal review — including the client agreement, which means a client saying yes tomorrow couldn't actually be signed or invoiced.
  • An audit of all of this was commissioned and written. You'll get it. The headline: yourco has built an extraordinary machine and has not yet run a business.

The pivot

The question you should be asking

If the product is this good, why has nobody bought it?

Ask it directly, because the answer is the reason this conversation is happening.

It isn't that people said no. Almost nobody has been asked. Three deals were sourced entirely through the Founder's own relationships and then went quiet — not because anyone objected, but because no one followed up and closed them. Zero cold emails have ever been sent. The audit — the paid front door the entire model rests on — has never once been delivered to a customer.

And underneath that: if someone had said yes last week, there was no signed client agreement, no business bank account, and no way to take the payment.

So the honest read is that yourco has a building problem — in the sense that building is the thing that kept happening instead of selling. The machine is roughly a year ahead of the business. Closing that gap is not a technical job.

That's the real subject of these six sessions, and it's why the last one is about what you'd own.
