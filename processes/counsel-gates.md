### Gate 13 — why it's here
The SaaS-replacement wedge's differentiating line is *"you own the asset, we operate the reliability"* — but yourco today operates the OS without client code-ownership being part of the model, and an ownership promise touches IP and could undercut recurring lock-in. **Ray** rules the IP/terms (what the client owns: workflow logic + data + portable export vs. the code itself; what yourco retains: the reliability layer + infra); **Polo** rules the retainer implication. **No ownership promise in any proposal until both rule.** The build-vs-rent *finding* (Audit teardown lens) is fine to surface now; only the ownership *promise* is gated. Also carries the wedge's clean-room guardrail (build from workflow spec, never the incumbent's code/name) — a legal-adjacent standing rule Ray should bless as the offering matures.

### Gate 12 — why it's here
The launch-gate is written as **"nothing external"** and blocks the entire GTM machine, but `processes/launch-gate.md` still has *what the gate is*, *the resolution condition*, and *last real update* unfilled — so **no one in this OS can say whether it blocks the Founder walking into a co-working space and answering AI questions for an hour**. It is 3+ weeks past its own estimate and drifting (the exact failure `agents/brett/premortem-2026-06-12.md` warned about).

**This is a scoping question, not a route-around.** The ask to counsel is one sentence — *"does this cover in-person, unbranded conversations with local business owners?"* — and it resolves in one of two useful ways: it unlocks three channels that need no site, no domain, no Instantly, and no sending reputation (i.e. usable *this month*, while everything else waits), or it confirms the wait honestly and we stop wondering. Either beats the status quo of an undefined blocker. **the Founder owns the answer** (agents read the gate, never guess it); Ray tracks the row.
