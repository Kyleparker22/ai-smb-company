# Employee Tiers & the Sophistication Spectrum

> **Owners:** Brett (strategy) · Polo (pricing) · Kimi (delivery) · Kolby (eval) · Kemba (template). The canonical model for *how sophisticated an employee is* and *whether it's truly an agent or an AI-assisted service* — so the OS reasons about scope, pricing, delivery, and reliability consistently. Strategy note, 2026-06-12.

## The two tiers
A product ladder that is also the pricing structure and the land-and-expand path.

| | **Tier 1 — "handle it"** | **Tier 2 — "produce it"** |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | answers, qualifies, books, reminds, logs, supports, invoices | **creates deliverables + runs multi-step pipelines** — copy, media, research, reports, campaigns |
| Examples | intake · scheduling · follow-up · Q&A · support · invoicing | listing-marketing · content-production · market-research · proposal-builder · onboarding-ops |
| Build + retainer | lower (the wedge) | **higher** (more value, more orchestration) |
| Reliability bar | high | **higher** — a bad deliverable hurts more than a double-book |
| Role in GTM | **the land** — easy yes, 48h, proven | **the expand** — Bird grows a trusted Tier-1 account into Tier 2 |
| Sequence | **first** | **after** the eval track record is earned (autonomy ladder) |

## The sophistication spectrum (what "more sophisticated" means)
1. **Front office** — intake, scheduling, follow-up, Q&A. *(Tier 1)*
2. **Back office** — invoicing, bookkeeping, support. *(Tier 1)*
3. **Production** — *creates* real deliverables: listing copy, social posts, comps/research, proposals, reports, edited media, data pipelines. *(Tier 2)*
4. **Orchestration** — runs a multi-tool pipeline end-to-end, coordinating several steps (incl. handing off human steps). *(Tier 2)*

Climbing the spectrum = more value, more margin, **more defensible** (harder to DIY) — and a higher reliability bar. The moat (eval, watchdogs, approval, accountability) matters *more* as you climb, not less. That's good: it's exactly where a no-code operator can't follow.

## The honest line: an *agent* vs. an *AI-assisted service*
The trap a lot of "AI agency" pitches fall into is selling a **human service with AI tools** as if it were an **autonomous agent.** Keep it straight:

- **Autonomous agent** — the value step is *digital* and can be done without a human in the loop (write the copy, pull the comps, draft the post, run the API, publish, follow up). yourco's product.
- **AI-assisted service** — a key value step needs a **human in the physical or creative loop** (walk the property with a phone for a 3D capture, shoot the photos, be on-site). The AI is a *tool*, not the worker.

**The test:** *"Can the core value step happen with no human present?"* If no, part of it is a service — name that step as the **human-in-loop** step the employee *coordinates*, scope + price it honestly, and don't pretend the agent does it. Many great Tier-2 employees are ~80% agent + a named human step (e.g. a listing employee that does everything *except* the physical capture, which it books + then processes/publishes the output of). That's fine — just be honest where the line is.

## What changes by tier
- **Pricing (Polo):** Tier 1 on the proven per-vertical references; Tier 2 priced to the *value of the deliverables it replaces* (a copywriter + social manager + coordinator), not the cost. Higher build + retainer. Per-employee `cost.md` watched harder (Tier 2 can be heavier).
- **Delivery (Kimi):** Tier 2 = more connectors + orchestration + named human-in-loop steps; the 48h promise still holds for a *working first slice*, with the pipeline maturing over weekly iteration.
- **Eval (Kolby):** Tier 2 gets a *harder* gate — deliverable quality, factual grounding (no fabricated comps/stats), brand voice, and the legal/scope landmines specific to the work. The autonomy ladder applies more strictly: Tier-2 deliverables stay human-approved longer.
- **Positioning (Pickle/Reilly):** lead with Tier 1 (easy yes); Tier 2 is the expansion + premium story. Sadie surfaces emerging Tier-2 service plays worth productizing.

## The rules
1. **Tier 1 first.** Land + prove reliability before climbing.
2. **Reliability scales with sophistication** — honor the autonomy ladder harder for Tier 2.
3. **Be honest about agent vs. service** — name the human-in-loop step; never sell a service as an autonomous agent.
4. **Don't sell sophistication you can't make reliable.** A great Tier-1 employee beats a flaky Tier-2 one.
