## Five blocks, in order

**A. The money map** — how a customer goes from never-heard-of-you to paid, and where they fall out.
**B. The time map** — what the owner does that isn't the work and isn't growth.
**C. The breakage map** — what falls through when they get busy.
**D. The readiness check** — what they already pay for, who else touches it.
**E. The control map** — what they will let an agent do on its own, and what it must never touch.

Full wording lives in the SOP. Run them in order; do not turn the call into a pitch.

## What you are listening for

Money leaking, time going, and **what only the owner can do**. The last one is usually the real
constraint and the owner rarely names it directly.

## Block E is the one you will want to skip

It feels like a governance conversation rather than a sales one, so it gets dropped for time. Don't.
Every answer has a destination: what it must never touch becomes the deny-list, what needs checking
becomes the R1 floor, and **"what would you need to see before you'd let it do this without asking?"
becomes the promotion criterion in their own words.** That last answer is the single most valuable
sentence in the call — it converts "trust us" into a condition the client set themselves.

Never say *autonomy*, *guardrail* or *governance* on the call. Ask it in the owner's language.

## Numbers, not adjectives

"Slow" and "a lot" are not findings. Get the count, the value, and the frequency, and show the
arithmetic in the report. Every dollar figure must be computed from the client's own inputs.
