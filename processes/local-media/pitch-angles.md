# Pitch angles — local press (staged; nothing sends until OtherVenture clears)

> Katie drafts, the Founder sends. Every draft passes `brand/writing-rules.md` + the rules in `founder-boilerplate.md` (no agent names, no prices, no unsourced stats, pre-revenue honesty — the glass-box facts are the story precisely because they're *true*). Personalize the first line to something the outlet actually ran; a pitch that could go to anyone goes to no one.

## Angle 1 — The glass box (the lead angle)
**"A St. Pete founder built a company that runs itself on AI — and now builds the same for local businesses."**
One human, a full roster of AI agents doing the sales ops, finance pulses, QA reviews, and morning briefings — 24/7, on infrastructure he owns, with an approval gate that keeps a human on anything that matters. The hook for a reporter: *this isn't a company selling AI, it's a company operated by it* — and everything it sells is something already running on itself. Local peg: built in St. Pete, aimed at Tampa Bay's service businesses (landscapers, contractors, clinics).
Short pitch (3 sentences): *I run a St. Pete AI consultancy where I'm the only human — the rest of the operating team is AI agents I built, running around the clock on our own infrastructure. Everything we sell a local business is something already running our own company first, approval gates and all. Happy to open the actual dashboards and show [outlet]'s readers what a one-person AI-operated company really looks like — the honest version, including what the AI is not allowed to do.*

## Angle 2 — The local-SMB stakes (teach-the-market)
**"Tampa Bay's service businesses are losing real money to missed calls — and the AI fix has quietly become affordable."**
The pain-point-inversion angle for a business desk: the missed-call/slow-follow-up leak in owner-operated service businesses, what changed in the last 18 months, and what owners should actually ask before buying anything (the audit-first argument, told as advice, not as an ad). Works as a contributed piece / op-ed if the outlet takes them.
Guardrail: every number in this pitch must carry a source from the last 12–18 months (`founder-boilerplate.md` §rules) — no evergreen folklore stats.

## Angle 3 — The contrarian reliability take
**"Most 'AI agents' are demos that die in production. Here's the part nobody sells."**
The moat POV as thought leadership: evals, approval gates, watchdogs, rollback — why the unglamorous reliability layer is the whole game, argued by someone who runs it live on his own company. Best for Inno/TBBJ-style analysis pieces or a podcast conversation; positions yourco without pitching it.

## Angle 4 — The chamber/trade version (member spotlight)
The glass-box story compressed to a member-spotlight format: local founder, what the business does in one sentence, why it started in St. Pete, one concrete "here's what an AI-run back office looks like" detail. No selling — the spotlight *is* the sell. Reusable verbatim across chambers and trade newsletters with one localized line each.

## What we never pitch
Client stories that don't exist yet (pre-revenue honesty — the company's own operations are the only case study until a client agrees in writing to be one) · fabricated or simulated endorsements (the advisory-panel exercise is internal-only, per the external-surface rules) · anything naming an agent persona (functions only, externally).
