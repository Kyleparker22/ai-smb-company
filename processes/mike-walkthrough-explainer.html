What yourco is, and where it actually stands.

A boutique AI-implementation company: we diagnose how a business really runs, then build and operate a custom AI system inside it. The client never touches a token, a model, or a server — they get an outcome. Everything below is real and checkable; the right-hand column of each section is the part most companies leave out.

The business

One product, one motion. Every engagement starts with an Audit that finds and prices the bottleneck; what we build is scoped from what the Audit found.

What's built

  • Audit first — a ~1-week diagnostic, priced, credited against the build
  • Then a custom AI OS — agents scoped to the 8 pillars of a business: intake, sales, marketing, customer, operations, back office, company brain, training
  • Four tiers by scope — Core · Suite · Operation · Command, $3–10k/mo
  • We absorb all model and infrastructure cost. Flat retainer, no metering

What's true

Zero audits have been delivered. The front door of the only motion we have has never been opened.

One deal sits at proposal — Sample Client, $1,000/mo against a $3,000 floor, unsigned since June.

Tier prices are a working draft, not a ratified sheet.

Why this is defensible

Agent tooling is commoditizing. Anyone with $20/month can wire a workflow. Almost nobody can prove the workflow did its job to the person whose name is on the building.

The moat

  • Reliability, evaluation, observability, approval, audit trail. We charge for the proof, not the automation
  • Autonomy is earned — every agent action starts behind a human gate and climbs only on evidence it works
  • The model-upgrade dividend — we own the stack, so every AI improvement is swapped in underneath the client free. What we sell appreciates; what they'd buy instead depreciates

What's true

The moat is a design, not a demonstrated fact. It runs daily on our own operation — real evidence of a real kind — but it is unproven across paying clients.

Until then it's the name of a hypothesis, held with conviction and stated as one.

The machine that runs it

yourco runs itself on the same kind of system it sells. That's the proof asset — we're the reference client.

Agents
27 named agents — sales, delivery, finance, marketing, trust and compliance — each with a defined scope, an approval rung, and its own Slack channel
Always-on runtime
A private server runs scheduled loops with no human: briefings, pipeline reports, eval reviews, consistency checks, a watchdog
The approval gate
Agents may draft, read and post. Sending, deleting and shell access are denied at the infrastructure level — always-on does not mean auto-send
Written memory
111 decisions with their alternatives and what would reverse them · 48 learnings · 18 reusable procedures
Self-checking
23 machine-checked invariants catch facts that drift between documents; decisions carry trip-wires that report their own expiry

It was switched off for twelve days. The runtime paused on 4 August and resumed on the 16th, after a five-day server outage and a stretch of API billing failures. Every loop skipped honestly and said so — but nothing outside the box noticed, and the watchdog was itself asleep for five weeks. Twelve loops haven't run successfully in over a month. That's the most important thing to know about the machine.
