## Part 1 — The thesis

AI intelligence is becoming a commodity. The models get better, cheaper, and more available to everyone
every few months, and no one who simply *uses* a model has an advantage from that.

What is not commoditizing is **deployment** — getting AI to do real work inside a real business,
reliably, in a way an owner will actually trust with their customers and their money.

That's the gap. Most small and mid-size businesses now know AI could run large parts of their operation.
Almost none of them can get from knowing that to having it running. They don't have the time, the
technical staff, or any way to judge whether what they built is safe to leave alone.

**yourco closes that gap as a service, not as software.**

## Part 2 — The moat: why this isn't just "someone who knows AI tools"

Anyone can wire up a chatbot. The tooling is free and getting easier. So the obvious question is what
stops a client — or a competitor — from just doing it themselves.

The answer is everything that happens *after* the thing works once:

| The layer | What it means | Why it's hard to copy |
|---|---|---|
| **Reliability** | It works on the 500th job, not just the demo | Requires building for failure, not for the demo |
| **Evaluation** | Measured proof that it's right, not a feeling | You have to build the scoring before you trust the system |
| **Observability** | You can see what it did and why | Logging, audit trails, replay |
| **Approval gates** | Nothing consequential happens without a human until it's earned the right | This is the trust unlock, and it's a discipline, not a feature |
| **Integration** | It plugs into the systems the business already runs on | Every business is different; this is real work |
| **Executive trust** | The owner sleeps at night | This is the actual product |

A no-code operator can build the first version. They cannot build this. **This layer is where the
margin lives**, and it's the reason the offering is a service yourco operates rather than software the
client is handed.

**One consequence worth understanding, because it's the standing answer to "won't this be obsolete in a
year?":** because yourco owns the stack and sells the *outcome*, every model improvement flows to the
client as a free upgrade. The offering appreciates as AI advances. Any tool the client could have bought
instead depreciates.

## Part 3 — What yourco actually sells

**The motion is: audit first → custom AI OS.**

**The audit.** Every engagement starts with a free diagnostic (free while yourco is getting started — 2026-08-16, `decisions/2026-08-16_audit-is-free.md`). yourco goes through the business, finds
where the time and money are leaking, and quantifies it. The client gets a real report whether or not
they buy anything else. If they do buy, the audit fee is credited in full against the build.

This exists because it solves the two hardest problems in this business at once: it gets paid for
discovery instead of giving it away, and it means nobody is ever sold a system before somebody
understood their business.

**The AI OS.** Then yourco builds and *operates* a custom multi-agent system fit to that business.
Not a tool the client has to learn. A system that does work, that yourco owns the reliability of.

The scope is mapped across eight areas of a business:

> **Intake · Sales · Marketing · Customer · Operations · Back Office · Company Brain · Training**

Each piece can ship in one of three shapes: a named "digital employee" with its own email in the
client's system, a headless automation nobody sees, or an AI feature embedded in the client's own
product for *their* customers to use.

**Four tiers**, by how much of the business it covers — **Core → Suite → Operation → Command.** Pricing
is drafted but not locked; we'll go through the real numbers in Session 5 rather than hand-wave them
here.

**The defining principle:** the client never touches tokens, models, or infrastructure. yourco absorbs
all of it. The client gets an outcome.

**Who it's for:** small and mid-size businesses — the segment large enough to feel the pain of manual
operations and too small to hire an AI team. Not restricted by industry.

## The question you should be asking

**"If the product is this good, why has nobody bought it?"**

Ask it directly, because the answer is the reason this conversation is happening.

It isn't that people said no. **Almost nobody has been asked.** Three deals were sourced entirely
through the Founder's own relationships and then went quiet — not because they objected, but because no one
followed up and closed them. Zero cold emails have ever been sent. The audit — the paid front door the
entire model rests on — has never once been delivered to a customer.

And underneath that: if someone had said yes last week, there was no signed client agreement, no
business bank account, and no way to take the payment.

So the honest read is that yourco has a building problem in the sense that building is the thing that
kept happening instead of selling. The machine is roughly a year ahead of the business. Closing that
gap is not a technical job.

That's the real subject of these six sessions, and it's why the last one is about what you'd own.
