## What you just earned

At R1 you can generate a **real, working demo** for a business you have just met — built around *their*
front desk, *their* vertical, *their* name. You walk into your dentist's office, say "watch this," and
minutes later that dentist is looking at a system that answers their phone.

This is the difference between you and every other person who has ever asked them for a meeting. You are
not pitching. You are **giving** — and you are giving something they can see working.

It is earned rather than handed out for two honest reasons: a generated demo carries yourco's name to a
stranger, and each one costs real build spend. Producing one referral that reached a real conversation is
the evidence that you will use it on real businesses rather than on curiosity.

## How to use it

1. **Have the conversation first.** The demo is the second half of a moment, never the opener. You need
   their business name, their vertical, and one real problem in their own words.
2. **Generate it.** One request, their business name, their vertical. yourco builds it from the same
   demo-kit every client engagement starts from — you never author what it says.
3. **Show it, don't narrate it.** Open it and let them click. The four screens walk themselves: what the
   agent handles, the approval screen, the status board, the monthly report.
4. **Ask one question at the end**, then be quiet: *"Would it be useful if that were answering your phone
   on Saturday?"*

## The thirty seconds before you open it

> "You know how you lose leads when you can't get to the phone? I work with yourco — they build a system
> that answers every call, qualifies the job, and books it. It never quotes a price or goes off-script;
> you approve anything a customer sees. I had them put together a quick one off your website. Want to
> look?"

Notice what that sentence does **not** do: it does not promise a number, it does not name a price, and it
does not claim anyone else's results. Keep it that way.

## The rules that come with it

- **No prices, ever.** Not in the demo, not in the conversation. yourco's tooling scans every generated
  page and refuses to build one containing a price — that is a hard stop in the software, not a habit.
  If they ask what it costs, the honest answer is the whole answer: *"It's scoped in the audit — that's
  the first real step, and it's a paid one."*
- **No invented results.** No metrics, no percentages, no "our clients see X," no testimonials. The
  generator refuses those too. yourco is early and says so; a demo that shows what the system *does* is
  stronger than a claim nobody can check.
- **Every page marks itself a demo** and names you as *an independent yourco referral partner — not an
  yourco employee*. That line protects you as much as it protects yourco.
- **Five demos per rolling week.** Not a bureaucratic limit — a quality one. If you are generating five a
  week and none of them become conversations, something about the conversation needs fixing, not the
  volume.
- **Every generation is logged.** You see it in your own history; so does yourco. That ratio — demos
  given to conversations started — is yours to be proud of, and it is the thing that eventually earns R2.

## The mistake to avoid

The demo is not the sale, and it is not proof of anything by itself. It is a door-opener that earns a
real conversation with yourco. Hand that conversation over cleanly and let the audit do the work — the
moment you start running discovery yourself, you have left your lane and taken on risk that was never
yours to carry.
