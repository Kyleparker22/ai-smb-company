## Why this lesson exists at R1

You reached R1 because a business you introduced had a real conversation with yourco. From here you
will be asked "so what actually happens next?" more often than you will be asked anything else. The
honest answer is short, and knowing it stops you from filling the silence with something invented.

## The path, end to end

1. **A short intake, filled in before the first call.** The business answers a handful of questions
   about how work reaches them, what they use, and what breaks.
2. **A diagnostic conversation (about an hour).** yourco maps three things: where money leaks, where
   the owner's time goes, and what only the owner can do.
3. **yourco does the analysis offline.** The bottlenecks get ranked, and the biggest one is quantified
   **in the business's own numbers, with the arithmetic shown.**
4. **A findings call, and a written audit report.** The diagnosis, the dollar cost of the top problem,
   and a prioritized roadmap of what to build first.
5. **A proposal for the first build** — and the audit fee credits toward it on a minimum engagement, so
   the audit reads as a first step rather than a toll.
6. **Build, go-live, and then yourco operates it** — weekly iteration, and the system keeps improving.
   You stay hands-off through all of it.

The audit is **paid** and it is the **mandatory front door** — every engagement starts there, including
warm introductions. That is not a hurdle yourco puts in front of your friend; it is how yourco learns
the business well enough to build something that fits it.

## The one thing worth explaining if they push back on paying for an audit

> "They diagnose it first and quantify what it's costing you in your own numbers. If they can't
> meaningfully help, they'll tell you that on the call — and the fee comes off the build if you go
> ahead."

That is true, including the part about telling someone there is nothing here for them. yourco's own
rule is an honest diagnosis: if it can't help, it says so on the call and does not sell.

## What "a real conversation" means for your ladder

Your rung moved because a referral reached a sit-down or an audit — a decision-maker actually engaged.
Registering a name does not do it, and a polite "sure, send me something" does not do it. That is
deliberate: the ladder measures what happened, not what was attempted, so nobody can talk their way up
it.

## Still not yours, at any rung on this page

Discovery, the diagnosis, the report, the price, and the close are yourco's, start to finish. Your job
after the introduction is to stay warm and stay out of the way — and to tell yourco if you hear
something they should know.
