## The whole motion, in five steps

1. **Join.** Sign the Partner Agreement, put a W-9 on file, get your referral link and code.
2. **Refer.** A warm intro is best; your link or code also works. The company is tagged to you in yourco's
   records at first contact — your attribution is locked from that moment.
3. **yourco takes it.** The audit, the proposal, the close, the build, the day-to-day. You stay hands-off.
4. **Credit.** The client signs and their first payment clears — now they are an *active referred client* on
   your book.
5. **You get paid.** The second Friday of every month, on revenue actually collected, at your current tier,
   on your whole book.

## What the intro sounds like

You are not pitching. You are connecting someone you already know to a company you already trust.

> "You know how you keep saying half your week disappears into calls and quotes? I know a company that builds
> an AI system that runs that for you — they audit your business first, build it custom, and operate it.
> Nothing sends without your sign-off. Want an intro?"

Then a three-way email or text to your yourco contact, or your link. That is the whole job.

## What you do

- Spot owners drowning in calls, follow-up, scheduling, or admin — people who would rather buy an outcome than
  learn a tool.
- Make the introduction.
- Tell the truth about yourco: audit first, built custom, operated for them, and nothing customer-facing goes
  out without their approval.

## What you never do

- **Quote a price, negotiate, or sign anything on yourco's behalf.** yourco owns the client relationship and
  the price. If a prospect asks what it costs: *"They scope it in the audit — I'll connect you and they'll
  walk you through it."*
- Promise results, timelines, or savings that are not in writing.
- Cold-spam. Your entire value is that you are warm.
- Run discovery, demos, delivery, or support. That is yourco's, start to finish.
