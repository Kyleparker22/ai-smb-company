> of income, and no bounty is owed or payable until the program launches and its legal review clears.

## Two ways to refer, and you can use both

**Introduce.** You put yourco in front of someone you know. You open the door; yourco takes it from
there. That is the whole job, and it is what the rest of your training covers.

**Submit.** You hand yourco the details and **yourco makes the approach**. You are not in the
conversation at all. This exists because there is a real gap between "I know someone who needs this"
and "I am comfortable introducing them," and losing the referral to that gap helps nobody.

Neither one is the lesser option, and neither one is your permanent type. Most connectors will do both
depending on how well they know the person.

## What a submission pays

- **$25** once yourco verifies the contact is a real, reachable business owner. We aim to review every
  submission within **24–48 hours**.
- **Another $25** if that contact books a real conversation with yourco.
- **The normal commission on top** if they become a client — a submission never costs you the
  commission you would have earned by introducing them.

Your console shows every submission you have made and exactly where it sits, including the ones that
did not work out. Nothing disappears quietly from that list.

## The two questions, and why they are not paperwork

Every submission asks you **how you know this person**, and whether **they are expecting to hear from
anyone**. You have to answer the first one; a submission without it cannot be verified.

Here is the honest reason. When you introduce someone, you are the one making contact and they already
know you. When you *submit* someone, **yourco is the one making the call** — to a business owner who
never asked to hear from us, using details we got from a third party. There are real laws about that,
they apply to yourco directly, and the only way we can make the call at all is if we can say where the
contact came from.

"They don't know yet" is a completely normal answer and it is not a problem — say so, and we will
approach them accordingly. **Guessing, or telling us what you think we want to hear, is the problem.**

## The one thing that could end this program

Do not submit names from a list you bought, scraped, downloaded, or copied out of somebody else's
system. Not one. Those get rejected, and if it were ever done at scale it would put the whole connector
program at legal risk — which is a strange amount of damage for $25.

A useful test: if you could not say one true, specific sentence about this business from your own
knowledge, it does not belong in the form.

## The rest of the rules

- **One referrer per business, ever.** If someone already submitted a business, the first submission
  holds it. The console tells you immediately rather than letting you find out at payday.
- **You cannot verify your own submission.** Someone at yourco reviews it, and their name goes on the
  record either way.
- **A rejected submission costs you nothing** and does not count against you.
- **yourco owns the conversation from there** — you do not quote a price, promise an outcome, or
  follow up on yourco's behalf. That is unchanged from everything else you do here.
