## What R3 actually unlocks

You may **run the diagnostic conversation yourself**, with yourco supporting you and reviewing what
comes out of it. You are not running the engagement — yourco still does the analysis, writes the
report, prices it, and approves it before a word of it reaches the business. What you gain is the room.

It is earned at R3 because by then you have three live referred clients whose retention is holding. You
have watched yourco do this several times, on businesses you brought, and the conversation is no longer
theoretical to you.

## The conversation, in order

Run these in this order and mostly listen. You are listening for three things: **where money leaks,
where the owner's time goes, and what only the owner can do.**

### A. The money map

1. Walk me through how a customer goes from "never heard of you" to "paid you." Where do they come
   from?
2. Roughly how many inquiries or leads a month? How many turn into customers?
3. What is an average job or customer worth to you?
4. Where in that journey do you *lose* people — and do you know why?

### B. The time map

5. What do you spend the most time on that isn't the actual work, and isn't growth?
6. What happens to a call or message that comes in while you are on a job, or after hours?
7. What is the task you most dread, or keep putting off?
8. If you cloned yourself, what would the clone do first?

### C. The breakage map

9. What falls through the cracks when you are busy — follow-ups, quotes, scheduling, invoicing?
10. What is something a customer complained about that was really an operations problem, not a quality
    one?
11. What is the bottleneck that, if it vanished, would let you take on more work today?

### D. The readiness check

12. What tools or software do you already pay for?
13. Who else touches these processes — just you, or a team?
14. If the number-one thing were fixed quickly, what would "it's working" look like to you?

**Question 3 and question 2 are the two you cannot leave the call without.** The whole diagnosis turns
on being able to multiply a leak by what a customer is worth, in their numbers. Get them, write them
down exactly as said, and do not estimate one on their behalf.

## The frame the findings are told in

You do not present the findings — yourco does. But knowing the shape of the story tells you what to
listen for, and it is why the questions above are the questions:

1. **Their business as one connected picture.** The things (a lead, a call, a quote, a job, an
   invoice, a customer) and the links between them. A bottleneck is a **broken link** in that picture,
   and the dollar cost is the cost of that one broken link.
2. **A system, not a tool — because the leak is in the links.** This is why the software they already
   pay for did not fix it: each tool works inside one box; the money leaks between boxes.
3. **And it compounds.** Everything the system does is written back, so month three is better than
   month one. That is the honest reason it is an operated engagement rather than a purchase.

Plain language only. Never reach for jargon to sound credible in front of an owner — it does the
opposite.

## The four guardrails

- **Honest diagnosis.** If yourco cannot meaningfully help this business, that gets said on the call
  and nothing is sold. This is not a soft rule; it is the posture the whole company is built on, and it
  is worth more than the deal you would have talked yourself into.
- **No fabricated numbers, ever.** Every figure comes from what the owner told you, and the arithmetic
  is shown. If you did not hear it, it does not go in.
- **You do not price it.** Not a band, not a range, not "probably around." Pricing is yourco's.
- **You do not send the report.** yourco writes it and approves it. Your name being on the
  conversation does not put your hand on the deliverable.
