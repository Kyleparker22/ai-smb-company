## Start here

Your whole job is describing yourco to somebody who trusts you. So before anything else — before the
intro script, before the commission — you need to be able to say what this company actually does, in
your own words, without exaggerating it. This lesson is that.

## What yourco builds

yourco builds and runs a **custom AI operating system** for a business: a coordinated set of AI agents
that answer the calls, chase the leads, draft the paperwork, and handle the busywork that eats an
owner's week.

Not software they have to learn. Not a subscription that sits unused. **A system yourco builds around
how that specific business already works, and then operates for them.**

The line that matters most, and the one people get wrong: **the client never touches tokens, models, or
infrastructure.** yourco owns the reliability, the security, and the ongoing improvement. The client
gets an outcome.

## How it sells: the audit comes first

yourco does not walk in and start building. Every engagement starts with a **audit** — a diagnosis
that finds and quantifies the single biggest bottleneck in the business, then maps what to build first.

That matters to you for a practical reason: **you are never introducing someone to a purchase.** You are
introducing them to a diagnosis. It's a much easier conversation, and it's an honest one — yourco won't
know what a business needs until it looks.

## Why it isn't "just another AI tool"

Everyone is being pitched AI right now. Here's the actual difference, in plain terms:

- **A tool** makes the owner responsible for making it work. yourco is **operated** — when something
  breaks at 2am, that's yourco's problem, not theirs.
- **Nothing goes out unchecked.** Anything a customer would see is approval-gated until it has earned
  trust through evidence. "AI running your business" is exactly the fear people have; the honest answer
  is that a human stays in the loop until the system has proven it doesn't need one.
- **It gets better instead of older.** Because yourco owns the stack, every improvement in the
  underlying models flows to the client as a free upgrade. A tool a business buys today depreciates.
  This appreciates. That's the answer to *"won't this be obsolete in a year?"*

## The part you must not overstate

**yourco is early.** There is no long client list, no case-study binder, no impressive percentage to
quote. Say so if you're asked — it costs you nothing and it buys you everything, because the person
across from you can tell the difference between a real answer and a rehearsed one.

You have three honest things to offer instead: a **working demo** of what the system does, a **paid
audit** that will tell them the truth about their own business, and **you** — a person they already
trust, who thought of them.

That's a stronger hand than a fabricated statistic, and it's the only hand you're permitted to play.

## If you remember one sentence

> "They audit your business, find where you're actually losing money, then build and run an AI system
> that fixes it — you approve anything customer-facing, and they own keeping it working."
