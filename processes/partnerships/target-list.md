# Channel partners — target list & qualification

> Who to recruit as referral/white-label partners, why each fits, and how to qualify. Owner: **Bird** (partnerships) + **the Founder**. Pairs with `partner-one-pager.html` + `rev-share-model.md`. **Internal-only for now** — build the list + the kit; outreach flips at launch.

## The thesis
The fastest path to leads for a solo, trust-based founder is to **borrow trust that already exists.** Every partner below already sells to yourco's exact SMB verticals, already has the relationship, and is missing an AI offering. We become their AI fulfillment arm. One good partner can out-produce months of cold outbound.

## The ideal-partner test (all four = recruit)
1. **Serves our verticals** — landscaping, dental, med-spa, home services, auto, restaurants, law, fitness, e-commerce, etc.
2. **Has an SMB book of business** — existing, trusting client relationships.
3. **No AI offering** (or a weak one) — a gap we fill, not a competitor.
4. **Cares about reputation** — they won't risk clients on a junk vendor, which makes our reliability moat a *selling point*, not a cost.

## Connector ICP — *individuals* with rolodexes (added 2026-07-21, `decisions/2026-07-21_connector-program-v11-strengtheners.md`)
The categories above are **firm-level channel partners** (rev-share-model economics). This section is the **connector program's** recruiting target: *individuals* on the 10/12.5/15 escalator. The v1 assumption — "each connector knows a few owners" — is fine but low-yield. The 10× version targets people whose **profession is already a hub of SMB owners**:

| Who | Why they're a hub | The tell they'll produce |
|---|---|---|
| **Bookkeepers / CPAs / fractional CFOs** | See the books — they *know* who's bleeding money on slow quoting, missed revenue, unpaid invoices | Talks about clients' operational pain unprompted |
| **Insurance / P&C agents** | Annual touchpoint with every local contractor; trusted-advisor status | Book skews to trades/home services |
| **Business bankers / SBA lenders** | Every growing SMB crosses their desk | Lends to owner-operators, not startups |
| **Equipment dealers / suppliers (beachhead trades)** | The counter guy knows every hardscaper in the county by first name | Customers linger to talk shop |
| **Trade-association / chamber / BNI staff & fixtures** | Professional connectors already — it's literally their role | Runs the room at mixers |
| **Business coaches / fractional COOs** | Hired to fix the exact pains yourco automates | Already recommends vendors as part of the job |

**Why the escalator fits them:** a bookkeeper with 8 referred actives sits at 12.5% earning real monthly income for intro-making — the 11+ → 15% tier is *built* for professionals with a hundred-name book, not for a friend with three.

**How to reach them (in order):**
1. **Start with your own vendors.** the Founder + Partner B each already *have* an accountant, an insurance agent, a banker, suppliers. Those are warm, and each one either becomes connector #1–5 or names someone who should be. The ask is one coffee.
2. **Give-first, like everything yourco does.** Lead by *solving their client's problem*, not pitching income: "next time a client complains they can't keep up with calls/quotes, send them this link — if it turns into anything, you get a cut monthly for as long as they stay." Optionally sweeten: the connector's *own practice* gets a free mini-audit so they've experienced the product they're vouching for.
3. **Go where they already gather.** Chamber mixers, BNI chapters, trade-association meetings, the supplier counter, local CPA-society events. One visit with the one-pager (`referral-recruitment-onepager.md`) beats fifty cold emails — these are relationship professions; recruit them the way they work.
4. **Ask every signed client** who their bookkeeper/agent/banker is — a thrilled client's own vendors are the warmest possible connector recruits (they'll hear about yourco from the client anyway).
5. **Qualify with one question:** "how many business owners do you talk to in a normal month?" Under ~10 → casual connector, fine. Over ~30 → white-glove them: personal onboarding, walk their book together, treat them like the partner they are.

**What NOT to do:** mass-recruit. Five well-placed professionals out-produce fifty casual sign-ups, and every connector speaks with yourco's reputation attached — the vetting *is* the moat protection.

**⚠️ CPA-specific referral compliance (Ray, before papering any CPA-firm referral).** CPAs carry profession rules the other connectors don't: **AICPA Code §1.520** requires a CPA to **disclose** a referral fee to the referred client and **prohibits** commissions/referral fees where the CPA does **attest** work (audit/review/compilation) for that client; the **FL Board of Accountancy** mirrors this. So comp CPA referrals as **disclosed** (non-attest clients) or **reciprocal/non-fee**, never a silent commission. Bookkeepers, coaches, and non-CPA advisors aren't bound by this. Full call: `decisions/2026-07-20_in-person-local-gtm.md`; ties into the counsel-gated referral program.

## Category 8 — why the ideal-partner test doesn't apply
The four-part test above assumes a partner **missing an AI offering** whose **SMB book** we borrow. A firm like **Mindgrub** (~200 people; NASA, FBI, US Navy, Geico, Dell, Under Armour; "MG AI Labs" + a Fractional AI Officer service) **fails criteria 1 and 3 outright** — they serve federal/Fortune-500, not our verticals, and their AI practice is strong, not a gap. Recruiting them on the standard pitch would be a category error.

**The thesis is the inverse, and it's the same wedge logic as yourco Care** (`decisions/2026-06-16_caregiving-dtc-offering.md`): the incumbent's *human* cost-to-serve makes the small end structurally unservable, so they turn it away. A 200-person firm with federal contracts **cannot profitably build for a four-person hardscaper** — that's arithmetic, not preference. AI-native economics remove exactly that cost, so their floor is our ceiling and there is **no overlap to compete over**.

**Test for category 8 (replaces the four above):**
1. Is their **minimum viable engagement** far above our ICP's budget? (If they'd take a $3k/mo client, they're a competitor, not a partner.)
2. Do they **receive** SMB inbound they decline? (No decline flow = no referral flow.)
3. Is there a **reciprocal** direction — do we ever surface work above *our* ceiling (enterprise, federal, deep custom software) that we'd pass up?
4. Would they put their name next to ours? (Same reputation logic as criterion 4.)

**The pitch (different from the 30-second one above):** *"You turn away small businesses every month because they can't carry your model. We're built for exactly those — operated, recurring, our reliability. Send us the ones you decline; we'll send you anything that outgrows us."*

**Comp note:** do **not** lead with the connector commission (10–15% ≈ $300–450/mo on a $3k OS) — that's noise to a firm at this scale and can read as insulting. This is a firm-level reciprocal relationship; if money is ever discussed, it's `rev-share-model.md`, not the connector escalator (`decisions/2026-07-06_advisors-connectors-taxonomy.md` — connectors are *individuals* earning commission).

**First named target: Mindgrub** (Sample Contact, Founder/CEO — the Founder met him **2026-07-15**). Relationship depth: **one meeting** — treat as a relationship to earn, not a resource to spend. CRM: contact `cyofty`. Triage: `decisions/2026-07-05_tool-triage.md` §Addendum 07-16 (Mindgrub).

## Category 9 — business brokers / M&A advisors (the exit-flip door)
Added 2026-07-29 (`decisions/2026-07-29_exit-flip-targeting-lane.md`). The standard ideal-partner test doesn't apply (they don't have an SMB *service* book — they have a *listings* book), so category 9 carries its own test, like category 8.

**The thesis:** a for-sale listing is the purest owner-drain intent signal there is, but listings are broker-anonymized — the broker IS the door. And the broker's standing problem is dead inventory: owner-dependent businesses that won't sell (industry rule of thumb: most small-business listings never close — verify before using in any external copy). yourco's two-sided pitch solves it in both directions: install the OS and the business either **sells for more** (owner-dependence is the classic valuation discount) or **doesn't need to sell** (the owner steps back; the broker gets a grateful referrer and our exit-ready clients later).

**Test for category 9:**
1. Do they carry **Main-Street listings in or near our verticals** (trades, home services, local service SMBs) — not just lower-middle-market deals?
2. Do they have **stale/expired listings** they'd rather rescue than write off? (No dead inventory = weaker need.)
3. Will they make a **pre-listing introduction** ("talk to these folks before we list / while we wait for offers") — the exit-readiness consult as part of their process?
4. Same reputation bar as everyone: would they put their name next to ours?

**The pitch (30 seconds):** *"A third of your listings won't sell because the business IS the owner. Send us those. We install an operated AI OS that takes the owner out of day-to-day — either the multiple comes back up and you close it, or the owner decides they don't need to sell and you've got a client who owes you both. And when our clients do get exit-ready, you're who we send them to."*

**Comp note:** same as category 8 — this is a firm-level reciprocal relationship; brokers earn on the transaction side (their listings closing, our exit-ready referrals), not the connector escalator. If rev-share is ever discussed it's `rev-share-model.md`. ⚠️ **Ray checks state business-brokerage referral/fee rules before papering anything** (broker referral compensation is regulated in some states).

**Guardrails (from the decision):** never pitch "walk away day one" (earned-autonomy framing only); no growth promises pre-proof; **no scraping listing platforms** (BizBuySell etc. are ToS-gated — manual reading + Sadie's compliant exit-signal class only); all outreach OtherVenture-gated like everything else.

## Category 10 — exit planners / succession advisors (the succession-readiness door)
Added 2026-08-17 (`decisions/2026-08-17_succession-three-play-map.md`). CEPA-credentialed exit planners, succession-focused CPAs/attorneys, and family-business advisors. Like 8 and 9, the standard test doesn't apply — their book is *owners planning transitions*, not an SMB service book.

**The thesis:** a succession plan has two halves. The legal/financial half (buy-sell agreements, estate structuring, valuation, tax) is THEIR licensed work — yourco never touches it. The operational half — *can this business actually run without its founder?* — is the half they cannot produce, and it is exactly the AI OS. The sourced 2026 tailwind: roughly half of ST small-business owners are 55+, and ~85% of those have **no succession plan** (Axios Raleigh, 2026-02-24). Every succession path — sale, family handoff, employee ownership, or keep-and-step-back — fails on the same rock: the business is the owner. We are the fix for the rock; they own everything else.

**Test for category 10:**
1. Do they serve **Main-Street / small-SMB owners** (not just $10M+ lower-middle-market)?
2. Do their plans **stall on operational owner-dependence** — the client who "isn't ready to transition" year after year?
3. Will they position the OS as **part of the plan** ("succession readiness"), introduced under their advice relationship?
4. Same reputation bar as everyone: would they put their name next to ours?

**The pitch (30 seconds):** *"Every succession plan you write has an operational assumption in it: that the business can run without the owner. Most can't, and that's not a legal problem — it's the reason your plans sit unexecuted. We install an operated AI system that takes the owner out of the day-to-day, on an earned-autonomy path you can show your client. Your plan finally executes; we never touch the legal or financial side — that's yours."*

**Boundary (hard):** yourco is not a succession advisor and never drafts the legal/financial plan — UPL/licensed territory, same posture as Conduit's draft-for-attorney-review line. We deliver operational succession readiness; the advisor owns the plan.

**Comp note:** same as 8/9 — firm-level reciprocal; if rev-share is ever discussed it's `rev-share-model.md`, and Ray checks the professional-responsibility rules on referral fees for attorneys/CPAs first (stricter than brokers in most states).

## How to qualify a partner (quick)
- Do they have **10+ active SMB clients** in our verticals?
- Is AI a **gap** they're being asked about but can't serve?
- Do they value **recurring** revenue (vs one-time)?
- Would they put their **name** next to the work? (reliability matters to them = good fit)
- Red flag: a competitor building their own AI, or a churn-and-burn shop that won't care about quality.

## The pitch (30 seconds)
"Your clients are asking about AI. You don't want to build or babysit it. We deliver a named AI employee in their business in 48 hours — reliability, evals, approvals, security all on us — and you earn a recurring share for the life of every account. Your brand, your relationship; our engine."
