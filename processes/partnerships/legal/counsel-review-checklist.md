## A. Pyramid vs. legitimate MLM — the core analysis

1. **Is compensation tied to real product sales, not recruiting?** Confirm that every dollar — direct commission (§4.1) and the downline override (§4.3) — is paid **only on collected revenue from real YourCo clients**, and that **nothing** is paid for the act of recruiting, enrolling, or for downline size itself. Does the agreement's language (§3, §4.3.2) hold up to scrutiny?
2. **The FTC "no compensation for the mere act of recruiting" rule** (the line from the FTC's MLM guidance / *Koscot* / *BurnLounge* framework): does the override survive it? Is there any path by which a Partner profits primarily from recruiting rather than from client revenue produced?
3. **No buy-in / no inventory / no pay-to-play** (§3): confirm there is genuinely no purchase, fee, or investment required, so the classic pyramid indicia are absent.
4. **Unlimited depth is the risk to bound.** Should the override depth be **capped** (e.g., `[[N]]` levels)? Should there be an **earnings cap** or a **per-client total-load cap**? Counsel sets the bound. What is the maximum defensible compounding load on a single client's revenue?
   - **The stacking math counsel should price (computed 2026-08-07):** the 1% override is flat at every depth **and every ancestor earns their own 1% separately** — a client recruited at depth *N* pays the direct commission (10–15%) **plus N × 1%**. At depth 10 that is ~25% of the retainer flowing out on one client. Unbounded depth = unbounded load.
   - **FOUNDER'S CALL (2026-08-07): NO DEPTH CAP.** A 2-level cap was proposed and **declined** — full-downline, unlimited-depth override is the intended design (`decisions/2026-08-07_override-depth-uncapped.md`). Counsel is therefore asked to price and defend **unlimited depth as designed**, not to choose a cap.
   - **The question for counsel becomes:** (a) is unlimited-depth defensible at all under FTC/*Koscot*/*BurnLounge* and `[[FL]]` + target-state law, given that 100% of payout is tied to real collected client revenue and $0 to recruiting? (b) If depth stays uncapped, **which non-depth guardrails carry the compliance weight instead** — a per-client total-load ceiling, an aggregate earnings cap, active-book qualification to earn override at all (e.g., must have ≥1 active client of your own), anti-stacking rules, mandatory income disclosure? (c) What is the maximum defensible total load on a single client's revenue, and what happens contractually when a deep tree exceeds it? (d) If counsel's answer is "unlimited depth is not defensible," what is the **minimum** change that preserves the design's intent — and the Founder decides from there.
4c. **⚠️ THE COMBINATION — new 2026-08-11, and now the second hard stop alongside depth.** Three of the Founder's decisions have to be priced **together**, because each was defensible partly by reference to the others and they no longer hold each other up (`decisions/2026-08-11_connector-program-v2.md`):
   - **(i) yourco now pays cash on two non-revenue events** — $25 when a submitted contact is verified as real and reachable, $25 when it books a real conversation — before a dollar of client revenue exists.
   - **(ii) recruiting moved R2 → R1**, and **the override is payable at R1 too**. The **active-book qualification is gone** — the very guardrail item 4b named as carrying the compliance weight in place of the depth cap the Founder declined.
   - **(iii) depth remains uncapped** (`decisions/2026-08-07_override-depth-uncapped.md`, unchanged).

   **What this costs us in the §A analysis, stated plainly:** the sentence the whole defence rested on — *100% of payout is tied to real collected client revenue and $0 to recruiting* — **is no longer true**, and there is now **no non-depth guardrail on the table**. Counsel is asked: **(a)** is the combination defensible under FTC / *Koscot* / *BurnLounge* and `[[FL]]` + target-state law? **(b)** Is a bounty for producing a *contact* meaningfully distinguishable from consideration for *enrolling*, given it is not paid for signing up and not paid for recruiting — or does any cash paid before revenue collapse that distinction? **(c)** If the combination fails, **which single element is cheapest to drop** to restore defensibility, and what does each buy? the Founder's stated preference order if forced: drop the bounty first, then the R1 override payability, keeping uncapped depth last. **(d)** Does a bounty capped per connector per month materially change the answer (the cap mechanism exists; the number is unset)?

   **⚠️ UPDATED 2026-08-11 (same day, later) — this is no longer a design question sitting in a spec.** At the Founder's direction the **connector packet the Founder actually shows prospective connectors now markets both changes**: `rep-packet.html` + `rep-packet.pdf` carry a **"Hand us a name, get paid before it closes"** card stating the **$25 verified + $25 booked-call** bounty and the 24–48h review, and the **"Build a team"** card now states recruiting opens once **one referral has reached a real conversation**. This is the same escalation the 2026-08-09 entries recorded for the downline card, and it changes 4c's character exactly as it changed gate #5's: **the terms are being presented to real people in a live-use artifact, not held in an internal draft.** Two consequences counsel should price: **(e)** a bounty *presented as available* — even with a staged banner — is closer to an offer than a bounty that exists only in code; does presentation alone create exposure before a dollar moves, and does the staged banner cure it or merely mitigate? **(f)** the packet is where an **income-disclosure statement** must be conspicuous (§C item 11) — with a named dollar figure now on the page, is the current banner sufficient, or does a stated bounty amount trigger disclosure obligations that the 10/12.5/15% escalator alone did not?
   **What remains as mitigation, and it is real:** the staged banner under the hero was **widened in the same pass** to cover every payment type — it now reads *"no payment of any kind — commission, downline override, or submission bounty — is owed or payable until the program launches,"* where it previously said only "no commission." It also now instructs, in the packet's own "You never" list, that a connector must **never submit a name from a bought, scraped, or copied list**. **Do not remove either while this item and gate #5 are open.**
5. **Threshold question for the Founder:** is the multi-level override worth the regulatory exposure at all, versus a **single-level** override (the prior v0 design) or **no override**? Counsel should price the risk so the Founder can decide. *(This is the decision the whole gate exists to inform.)*

## D. Classification, tax & payments

13. **Independent-contractor classification** (§1.2): does the connector relationship hold as 1099 contractor (not employee) under federal and `[[state]]` tests? Any state (e.g., ABC-test states) where connectors would be reclassified as employees?
13a. **Company email + company tools for connectors (the Founder raised 2026-08-07).** the Founder wants connectors provisioned with an yourco email address and a Connector Console login. Counsel: (a) does issuing an **`@yourco.com` mailbox** to a 1099 referral partner materially worsen the classification analysis (company email/tools/integration are recognized indicators, and it contradicts the "not an yourco employee" language in the agreement, packet, and every generated demo)? (b) Is a **visibly-partner domain** (`@partners.yourco.com`) meaningfully safer, or is any company-issued address the same problem? (c) Is a **partner-portal login** (the Connector Console) neutral, as expected? (d) Same question for the **free digital employee** given at R1 and the **referral-spotter** at R0 — company-provided *tools* are a classification factor; does the Connector OS's whole give-them-agents design need contractual framing (e.g., licensed tools, revocable, no exclusivity, they may refer for others) to stay clean? **FOUNDER DECIDED 2026-08-07 — this is now a review-the-package question, not a choose-an-option question.** Classification: **independent contractors, treated like team**. Every connector gets an `@yourco.com` mailbox and Slack access from onboarding (one organized channel of record; prospects hear from an yourco address so the introduction looks unified). Counsel: given that decision, (e) **what must the Connector Agreement say** so the tools don't defeat the contractor position — presumably that email/Slack/console/spotter/demo-generator are *licensed, revocable, non-exclusive tools provided for convenience, conferring no employment* — and (f) **what must remain true in practice** (no set hours, no minimums, no supervision of time, free to refer for others)? The exposure here is **inconsistency**, not the choice itself: every surface still reads "an independent referral partner, not an yourco employee," and the lived arrangement must match that sentence. Flag anything in the Connector OS (yourco email, Slack, training that gates advancement, rung-based permissions) that reads as **control** rather than **support** — training that is *required to advance* is the newest and least-tested factor.
14. **1099 / W-9 mechanics** (§5.2): confirm reporting thresholds and the W-9-before-payout rule. ⚠️ **Rewritten 2026-08-13** — the flat Type-1 credit is retired and a **referring client now earns the same commission as a connector**, delivered as a bill credit up to their own bill and **in cash above it** (`decisions/2026-08-13_one-referral-rate-card.md`). So the question is no longer "is a $100 credit reportable": it is **(a)** is the credit portion a non-reportable price adjustment while the cash portion is 1099 income — i.e. can one obligation be split by delivery method? **(b)** does receiving cash convert a client into a 1099 contractor for the whole arrangement, including the credit half? and **(c)** a churned client is paid **entirely** in cash, so the answer cannot rely on them being a customer. `[[Counsel: characterisation of credit-vs-cash, and whether the credit half must be described as a discount rather than a commission.]]`
15. **Clawback enforceability** (§6): is offset-against-future-payouts plus repayment-on-notice enforceable in `[[state]]`? Is the `[[60]]`-day window defensible?
15a. **Payee entity — individual vs. the connector's LLC (the Founder asked 2026-08-07).** yourco's proposed policy: **pay whoever the W-9 names — individual or the connector's own entity, their choice, never required, never formed or paid for by yourco.** Counsel confirm: (a) does allowing entity payees change 1099-NEC treatment (single-member LLCs are still reportable) or backup-withholding duties? (b) does an entity payee **help or hurt** the independent-contractor posture in `[[FL]]`/ABC-test states — i.e., is it evidence of an independent business, or irrelevant? (c) **critically — could ever *requiring* (or subsidizing) entity formation be read as a pay-to-play/required-purchase, undermining the §3 "no buy-in" defense that anchors §A?** (d) is a short factual "getting paid" note in the packet (W-9 before payout · 1099-NEC in January · quarterly estimates are yours · ask your CPA whether an LLC/S-corp makes sense at your income level) safe, or does it stray into tax advice yourco shouldn't give?

## E. Outreach compliance (connector conduct)

16. **CAN-SPAM** for any connector email outreach: which obligations bind the *connector* vs. YourCo? Should YourCo mandate approved templates and prohibit independent bulk email?
17. **TCPA + state analogues (FL FTSA, DNC):** connectors making calls/texts — what consent regime applies, and how does the agreement (§8.1) allocate that risk? Should connectors be barred from calls/texts entirely?
17a. **⚠️ SOURCER MODE INVERTS THIS ENTIRE SECTION — new 2026-08-11.** Items 16–18 were written assuming the *connector* does the outreach and asking how YourCo limits its exposure to what they do. The **Sourcer** referral mode (`decisions/2026-08-11_connector-program-v2.md`) reverses it: the connector submits a business owner's name and contact **with no introduction**, and **YourCo makes the approach**. That is better for agency/vicarious-liability exposure and **worse for consent** — YourCo is now the caller, the emailer, the texter, on a person who never asked to be contacted and whose details came from a third party.
   Counsel: **(a)** what consent regime applies to YourCo's own first contact with a submitted business owner — TCPA + FL FTSA for call/text, CAN-SPAM for email, B2B carve-outs where they exist? **(b)** Is the DNC/FTSA analysis different for a business line vs. an owner's mobile, which is what a connector will usually hand over? **(c)** What must YourCo record at submission time to defend the contact later — the console currently **requires** *how the connector knows this person* and captures a tri-state *does the person know you are passing their details on* (yes / no / **unknown**, and "unknown" is left as itself rather than collapsed into either answer). Is that the right record, and is anything missing? **(d)** Does a submission where consent is `no` or `unknown` need a different first-contact channel or script than one where the owner was told? **(e)** Does paying for the contact (item 4c) change the analysis — is YourCo now *buying leads*, with whatever that triggers, and does the answer differ if the connector is paid on verification vs. only on a booked call? **(f)** What must the Connector Agreement say to prohibit bought, scraped, or list-sourced contacts, and to put the consequences of a false provenance answer on the connector?
18. **Vicarious liability:** can YourCo be held liable for a connector's spam/TCPA violation under an agency theory despite the independent-contractor label? How is that risk mitigated (indemnity §10.3, conduct rules, approved-channel-only)? **Note the split after 2026-08-11:** this question now covers *Introducer*-mode conduct only; for *Sourcer* mode the exposure is YourCo's own and direct (17a), not vicarious.
