# Back-office tool for sportsbook "agents" (pay-per-head bookmakers)

- **Proposed by:** the Founder, 2026-08-16 — organization, accounting, reminders, specials, player outreach for guys running books; explicitly *not* replacing their site; $50–100/mo small agents, $200–300/mo bigger ones; "easy build, low monthly cost for us."
- **Rejected:** 2026-08-16. **Three independent reasons — any one is sufficient, and only the first is about legality.**
- **Why:**
  1. **The customer's core business is the offence.** The described buyer — a small "agent" who already rents *their site*, carries a book of "people," and runs "specials" — is the **pay-per-head bookmaker** market, not a licensed operator. In the US, booking sports action outside a licensed sportsbook is illegal bookmaking in nearly every state, with federal exposure under **18 U.S.C. §1955** (illegal gambling business) and **§1084** (Wire Act), plus money-laundering risk on the cash handling. Software built *specifically* to make that operation run better is facilitation, not a neutral tool. This is not a counsel gate that clears — unlike TCPA or the MLM override, there is no compliant configuration of the same product, because the illegality is the customer's day job rather than a channel we picked.
  2. **It poisons the rails yourco is standing up this week.** Stripe prohibits unlicensed gambling businesses; a gambling-adjacent MCC risks a frozen account and can take the business banking with it — under **the same EIN** (`YourCo LLC`) that the Audit and every retainer would run through. It also destroys the executive-trust/procurement story that *is* the moat: "we also build tooling for bookmakers" is unanswerable in a dental or legal-vertical diligence conversation.
  3. **The economics are the parked model, and would be even if it were legal.** $50–300/mo, self-serve, "easy build and low monthly cost for us" is a restatement of [[2026-06-16_self-serve-saas]] — rejected because serve-yourself moves the eval risk onto the customer, which is the work yourco is paid to own. It is also **~30× below the Core floor** ($3,000/mo) and inverts `pricing/v0/os-tiers.md`'s explicit no-inversion rule.
- **What was RIGHT in it (keep this — it is the reusable half):** the Founder correctly identified a **shape**, not just a vertical: a cash-heavy, relationship-driven micro-business run from a phone and a spreadsheet, where the owner's whole day is chasing people, tracking who owes what, and running promos to keep them active. That is an excellent AI-OS customer — and the feature list named (organization, accounting, reminders, specials, outreach) maps almost one-to-one onto the 8-pillar taxonomy in `processes/ai-os-modules.md`. **Point the same build at a legal vertical with that shape.** Closest structural analogues: route businesses (vending, ATM, towing, mobile detailing, pressure washing) and appointment/deposit businesses — **three of which are already warm names on the Board tonight: Sample Contact (salon), Sample Contact (staffing), Sample Contact (painting).**
- **Revisit if:** yourco is pitching a **licensed** operator — a state-licensed, tribal, or offshore-licensed sportsbook with a compliance function — which is a different buyer, a different price point (six figures, not $100/mo), and a different product. The adjacent *legal* markets in this space (handicapper/tout subscription services, fantasy and pick'em contest operators) are real but small; they would need their own triage on merit, not this one's.
- **Check:** `_none — "is this buyer licensed" is a fact about a specific prospect, not something the repo can compute. Deliberately prose-only._`
