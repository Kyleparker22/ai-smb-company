## The copy (one page, sponsor-facing)

**Fund the first AI employee for every business in your portfolio.**

You already hold a portfolio of local businesses: your borrowers, your policyholders, your members, your franchisees. When one of them misses invoices for a month because the office manager quit, that's your problem too. A healthier portfolio pays back, renews, and stays.

Patronage is a sponsored program. You fund the first working AI module for a defined set of businesses in your portfolio. yourco builds and operates each one: a named digital employee with its own mailbox in that business's systems, answering intake, keeping scheduling and invoicing moving, with a human approving anything that goes out. Each business opts in, signs its own agreement, and owns its own relationship with us from day one. Declining costs them nothing with you, and the invitation says so.

Here's why this works where sponsored software never has. Banks have bundled SaaS discounts, chambers have handed out coupon codes, and the seats sit unused, because a license is not an outcome. What you're funding here is operated. There is no login for the business to ignore. The agent shows up working, and every quarter you get a portfolio report showing, in aggregate, what's live and what it produced.

One thing we want to be plain about before you ask: **you will never see any individual business's data.** Your report is portfolio-level only. No per-business metrics, no "which of my borrowers is struggling," not in the report and not verbally. Each business's data belongs to that business. We built the reporting layer to enforce this, and it's in the contract. If portfolio visibility is what you're shopping for, we're the wrong vendor.

Your name appears exactly twice: in each business's onboarding welcome ("your first module is sponsored by [institution]") and in your own marketing of the program to your portfolio. Never inside the product, never in front of their customers. You may name the program; you don't brand the agent.

**Packages** (illustrative shape; final pricing in a proposal, per portfolio):

| Tier | Sponsored businesses | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot | ~5–10 | Funded first modules + quarterly portfolio report |
| Portfolio | ~25–50 | Same, with batched-onboarding pricing |
| System | Franchisor-scale | Negotiated |

A sponsorship covers each business's first module for a defined term. After that, each business decides for itself whether to continue, expand, or stop, at standard rates, with no economics flowing to you and no visibility for you beyond the aggregate continuation rate.

If this fits how you think about your portfolio, the next step is a short conversation about which businesses would be in the pilot.

— the Founder, yourco · founder@yourco.example.com
