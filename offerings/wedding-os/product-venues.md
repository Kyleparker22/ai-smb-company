# Product 3 — Wedding OS for Venues (B2B)

> Shared engine, agents, autonomy rungs and legal fences: [`SPEC.md`](SPEC.md). This file covers only
> what is different about selling to a venue or venue group.
>
> **Sequence: build this second.** Biggest ticket, best retention, and — the strategic point — **a venue
> is a distribution channel to couples**, which is what makes product 1 viable later.

---

## The buyer

A wedding/event venue running **50–150 weddings a year**: barn and estate venues, hotels with event
space, country clubs, dedicated event centres, winery and vineyard venues. Decision-maker is the owner,
GM, or **Director of Events / Catering**.

Bigger and more sophisticated than a planner: real revenue, sometimes a small events team, often
existing software, and occasionally procurement. Longer sales cycle, larger contract, better retention.

**Their job-to-be-done:** *"We're leaving bookings on the table because we're slow to respond, and
every booked wedding eats coordination hours we're not paid for."*

---

## The #1 lever — inquiry response speed

This is what makes the venue sale materially easier than the planner sale, and it should lead every
conversation.

**Venues receive heavy inbound and respond slowly.** Inquiries arrive nights and weekends — exactly
when the events team is working an event and not at a desk. A couple inquiring at 9pm Saturday to six
venues books a tour with whoever replies first.

This matters because it is:

- **A revenue number, not an efficiency number.** "We booked more weddings" beats "we saved hours" in
  every buying conversation.
- **Already measured, or trivially measurable.** Inquiries → tours booked → weddings closed is a funnel
  they can see. The before/after is unambiguous.
- **The Intake pillar, which is the easiest thing to automate well** — fast, templated, availability-
  aware first response plus tour booking.

**This alone would justify the retainer**, before any coordination value. It is also the cleanest
possible entry module: bounded, measurable, low-risk, and no vendor negotiation involved.

Secondary levers, in order: tour-to-booking conversion · coordination labour per booked event ·
preferred-vendor coordination · final-headcount and BEO chasing.

## What's different about the product

| | |
|---|---|
| **The pipeline is the primary surface** | Inquiry → tour → proposal → contract → deposit → booked → coordination. Product 1's planning board is downstream of this and secondary. |
| **Availability is the core datum** | Date availability drives everything: the first response, the tour offer, the hold, the upsell to an off-peak date. |
| **Off-peak yield management** | A genuine revenue play — steering flexible couples to Fridays, Sundays and off-season dates the venue wants to fill. Direct, attributable margin. |
| **Preferred-vendor coordination** | Venues hold vendor lists; the OS coordinates against them. See ⚠️ below. |
| **Non-wedding events broaden it** | Corporate, galas, mitzvahs, reunions. The same engine fills the calendar year-round — and widens the sale beyond weddings. |
| **Multi-property** | Venue groups price per property; the knowledge base compounds across the portfolio. |
| **They own the approval gate** | Their events team reviews R1 items. yourco operator cost stays low. |

⚠️ **Preferred-vendor economics need care.** Some venues take commissions or kickbacks from preferred
vendors. The OS must not obscure that from the couple, and must never be positioned to the couple as
neutral advice while steering toward a commissioned vendor. **Flag it in discovery and put it to
counsel** — this is a disclosure question, and it sits badly with the aligned-incentives positioning
that product 1 depends on. If the two products ever touch the same couple, this becomes a live
conflict.

## The strategic point — venues are distribution

Every venue books 50–150 couples a year, each of whom **has just locked their date and is about to
plan everything else** (`SPEC.md` §3 — venue-booked is the pivot).

That makes a venue partnership:

- **Warm, pre-qualified, near-zero-CAC distribution for product 1** at the exact moment of maximum need
- **A value-add the venue can offer** rather than something yourco has to buy
- **Vendor-market density** in one metro, compounding the §7 knowledge base fastest

**This is the answer to product 1's CAC problem**, and the reason venues are sequenced ahead of
couples. The conflict flagged above must be resolved by counsel before the two products are pointed at
the same couple.

## Pricing — the largest of the three

Real revenue and 50–150 events a year support a higher band than a solo planner.

| Rung | Scope | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **On-ramp** | Inquiry response + tour booking only — the #1 lever, nothing else | **$2,000–2,500/mo** |
| **Suite** | Adds the coordination layer for booked events, vendor coordination, BEO/headcount chasing | **$5,000–6,500/mo** |
| **Operation** | Adds sales/marketing, multi-property, non-wedding events | **$7,000–8,500/mo** |
| **Groups** | Per property, with portfolio pricing | Polo |

Sits cleanly on the existing ladder. No inversion problem, no ruling required.

**The ROI arithmetic is unusually clean here** — and it should be run in their numbers, not ours: *if
the venue's average wedding is $18,000 and faster response converts three additional weddings a year,
that's $54,000 against a $30,000 retainer.* One extra booking often covers a quarter. **Never quote
that with invented numbers** — use their inquiry count, their close rate, their average event value,
and show the math (audit SOP rule).

## Incumbent software — overlay, do not displace

Venues commonly run **Tripleseat, Perfect Venue, Event Temple, Planning Pod, or a hotel PMS/CRM**.
These hold contracts, BEOs, room blocks and financials.

The build-vs-rent teardown lens applies — **but the teardown's own qualification filter disqualifies
these from replacement.** They are systems of record. Replacing one loses their history and burns the
relationship.

**Therefore: overlay.** The OS does the work — responds, chases, coordinates — and writes back into
their system of record. Positioning is *"it makes your Tripleseat actually get used,"* never *"replace
your Tripleseat."*

⚠️ **Integration is the technical risk that could sink this product.** These platforms have varying and
sometimes poor API access. **Confirm integration feasibility for the top two before committing** — if
the OS can't write back, the venue gets double entry, which is worse than nothing.

## Risk profile

- **§6.1 sender liability** — low. The venue is the sender, on their own domain, to vendors they
  already work with.
- **§6.7 vendor relations** — largely dissolves, and inverts favourably: the venue has *power* in that
  vendor relationship, and vendors want to stay on the preferred list.
- **§6.4 UPL fence** — unchanged.
- **Couple-facing communication is the venue's brand.** White-label, mandatory. A bad automated message
  to a couple who has paid a $10,000 deposit is a serious incident.
- **Seasonality** — weddings cluster hard. The system sees extreme load peaks; capacity and cost
  modelling must assume the peak, not the average.
- **New: multi-stakeholder sale.** Owner, GM, Director of Events and sometimes a corporate parent.
  Longer cycle, more ways to stall. Budget for it.

---

## What must be true

1. **Inquiry response speed is genuinely costing them bookings** — and they'll believe it. If their
   response is already fast, the lead lever disappears and the sale gets much harder.
2. **They'll let an AI touch inbound leads.** Their most valuable asset at their least controlled
   moment.
3. **Integration with the incumbent system of record is technically possible.** *Gating — verify early.*
4. **The preferred-vendor conflict is resolvable** without compromising product 1's positioning.
5. **The longer sales cycle is affordable** given yourco's $0 cash position. This is not a fast first
   client — which is precisely why planners come first.

## Phase 0 questions for venue event directors

**The full field guide is [`phase0-venue-interviews.md`](phase0-venue-interviews.md)** — recruiting,
interview discipline, all 16 questions with listen-fors, the tally sheet, the ROI math, and
pre-committed kill criteria. Single source; no second copy of the questions lives here.

Three things from it that belong in this spec, because each can stop the product:

- **Question 4 is the whole interview** — ask them to open their inbox and read the last five inquiry
  reply timestamps aloud with you. Self-reported response time is flattering; the inbox is not. And
  they *discover* the number rather than being told it.
- **⚠️ No mystery-shopping.** The obvious move — a fake inquiry, timed — is barred by
  `offerings/secret-shopper/SPEC.md` §4: no probe of any kind, *including "harmless" form fills*,
  before the counsel-blessed protocol exists.
- **Question 9 is the hard stop.** If the dominant system of record has no usable API, the OS can't
  write back, the venue gets double entry, and that is worse than nothing. It is the one failure that
  cannot be positioned around.
