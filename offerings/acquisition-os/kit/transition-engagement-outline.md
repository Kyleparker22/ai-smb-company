# Transition-engagement outline — Acquisition OS (frontier #14)

> **STAGED — internal until launch (OtherVenture) + this offering's own trigger** (first mature engagement OR first warm ETA-community intro). This is the delivery outline Janice runs a real transition from; the consent-pack and transition-agreement drafts it references ride the gate-#1 counsel batch before any real engagement.

One contract, four phases, keyed to the deal's own dates. Buyer engages yourco; seller grants access pre-close (tri-party structure per SPEC §5).

## Phase 0 — Pre-close consent (gate, not paperwork)

Nothing is observed until this phase closes. Deliverables:

- **Seller consent pack signed:** written, scoped (which systems, read-only), revocable (revocation stops capture same-day), with deal-contingent destruction terms — deal dies → capture artifacts destroyed, confirmed in writing.
- **Employee consent where applicable:** no employee's exhaust is captured without that employee's own per-role consent under the standard #7 pack. Owner-role capture doesn't sweep in staff by side effect.
- **NDA/confidentiality** for pre-close deal context; deal documents the buyer shares are used for scoping only.
- **Recording posture set:** FL two-party consent — explicit consent at interview start, or written-only capture.
- **Scope memo:** which day-one modules ship at cutover (intake, scheduling, invoicing drafts — decided now, not at closing week).
- Hard boundary, stated to both parties: yourco is not a deal participant. No valuation opinions, no diligence conclusions, no advice on whether to close. Capture artifacts state what was observed; the parties and their advisors own the deal. We decline "quiet diligence-by-agent" requests flatly.

## Phase 1 — Capture (LOI → close, typically weeks–months)

- Understudy machinery (#7) pointed at the owner role: scoped read-only access to operational exhaust + structured interview cycles.
- Company Brain compiled: pricing logic, vendor relationships and their history, customer tribal knowledge, seasonal rhythms, unwritten workflows, the "what only the owner knows" list worked down item by item.
- **Capture-only, read-only.** No agent acts, or even drafts, in the business pre-close (SPEC §8). The business runs exactly as before through closing.
- Billing: capture phase billed as incurred; balance contingent on close (stated up front).
- If the deal dies: stop same-day, destroy per Phase 0 terms, confirm in writing, close the engagement cleanly.

## Phase 2 — Cutover (close ± days)

- Brain finalized to close-date state.
- Day-one modules live **in the buyer's tenant, under the buyer's authority** — the first agent action happens after close, not a minute before.
- Everything R1: the buyer approves every outward action. Day-one autonomy is not offered at any level of buyer enthusiasm; high-stakes actions (payments, pricing commitments, HR) hold at R1 regardless.
- Staff-facing communication runs from the cutover runbook: the agents are introduced to the team by the new owner as tools carrying the routine, never framed as surveillance or replacement of staying staff.
- White-label throughout: everything staff and customers see carries the business's brand, through both ownerships.

## Phase 3 — 90-day stabilization (close → day 90)

- Standard delivery loop at transition cadence: weekly iteration with the new owner, the approval queue doubling as their guided tour of the operation.
- Autonomy earned per action on eval evidence as the buyer's trust and the record accrue — the house matrix, experienced as the onboarding curve.
- The Brain keeps compounding under the new owner; "what only the owner knew" items get closed out as they surface in live operations.
- Day-90 checkpoint: stabilization review against the Phase 0 scope memo, then conversion to the standing OS retainer and the normal expansion path. Long-run note for the buyer's horizon: a mature engagement here manufactures the Exit-Asset (#3) conditions for the *next* sale.

## Asset split (standing, all phases)

Company Brain, handbooks, and captured knowledge are client-owned and convey to the buyer permanently; yourco's template/platform layer is licensed under the retainer (the #3 rule). A buyer who cancels keeps the Brain.
