# Acquisition OS — Build Spec

**Working name:** Acquisition OS (frontier #14)
**Author:** the Founder
**Stack:** the golden client template + the Understudy machinery (#7 — shadow loop, handbook schema, consent pack) aimed at the *seller* · Company Brain (pillar 7) as the capture target · standard moat layer (eval · approval · audit log) · diligence-adjacent artifacts reuse the Exit-Asset dossier schema (#3)
**Status:** Spec — see `offerings/_frontier-roadmap.md` row #14. Build trigger: **first mature engagement OR first warm ETA-community intro, whichever first.**
**Pillar / form factor:** Company Brain (pillar 7) as the spine, with day-one operating modules in any of the three form factors; sold as a transition project + standing OS retainer.

---

## 1. Concept

The operating rail for **search-fund / ETA / micro-PE buyers** of small businesses. The worst moment in every SMB acquisition is the ninety days after closing: the retiring owner — who *was* the operating system — walks out with thirty years of undocumented judgment, and a first-time owner-operator inherits a machine with no manual, no continuity, and staff watching to see if the new boss knows anything. Acquisition OS attacks that moment from before it happens: **pre-close, with the seller's documented consent**, yourco runs structured knowledge capture on the retiring owner — the Understudy machinery (#7) pointed at the most important role in the building — compiling pricing logic, vendor relationships and their history, customer tribal knowledge, seasonal rhythms, the unwritten workflows, into the **Company Brain**. At close, the buyer doesn't just get keys and a QuickBooks file; they get a working brain and, from day one of new ownership, **agent-carried operations**: intake answered, scheduling running, invoicing moving — the routine machine holding steady while the new owner learns the business instead of drowning in it.

This is the **buy-side twin of the Exit-Asset OS** (#3, `offerings/exit-asset-os/SPEC.md`): #3 makes an yourco-run business *worth more and transferable* at sale; #14 makes an acquired business *runnable* from the first morning of new ownership. Together they put yourco on both sides of every SMB sale — the demographic wave of retiring boomer owners is the largest ownership transfer SMBs have ever seen, and both directions of it are addressable with the same machinery. An Exit-Asset client's buyer is a natural Acquisition OS client; an Acquisition OS engagement matures into an Exit-Asset dossier for the *next* sale. One rail, both directions.

## 2. Why it's never been done

The transition-risk problem is universally known in the ETA world — searchers' own literature obsesses over the first 100 days and seller-transition clauses — and the standard mitigations are all human and all evaporate: a 6–12-month seller consulting agreement (the seller checked out the day the wire cleared), a transition binder (stale on arrival), or hiring an operator (the exact payroll a leveraged searcher can't carry). Nobody has ever *captured the owner into a system that then operates*, for three structural reasons: (1) capture was interview-hours economics — a consultant transcribing a founder's head cost more than the deal could bear and produced a document, not an operation; (2) there was no runtime for the captured knowledge to *do* anything — a binder can't answer the phone; (3) nobody serving this niche owns a reliability layer that would let a buyer trust agents with live operations in week one. yourco has all three inverted: the Understudy shadow loop makes capture a scoped-access process instead of an interview marathon; the golden template makes the captured knowledge operational (the Brain feeds working agents, day one); and the moat layer (R1 approval gates, eval, audit log) is precisely what makes "agents run the routine while the new owner learns" a responsible offer instead of a reckless one. The buyer ICP seals it (§3.3): ETA buyers are the rare SMB-adjacent customer that is findable in named communities, already funded, culturally AI-curious, and operationally desperate on a known date — the closing.

## 3. Build shape

### 3.1 The transition engagement (three phases, one contract)

| Phase | Window | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Capture** (pre-close) | LOI → close (typically weeks–months) | Seller consent pack signed (§5) → Understudy machinery on the owner role: scoped read access to operational exhaust + structured interview cycles → Company Brain compiled: pricing logic, vendor/customer relationship notes, workflow inventory, seasonal calendar, "what only the owner knows" list worked down. Deal-contingent: if the deal dies, capture stops and artifacts are destroyed per the consent pack |
| **Cutover** (close ± days) | Closing week | Brain finalized to close-date state · day-one modules go live in the buyer's tenant (intake, scheduling, invoicing drafts — scoped in Capture) · all R1 approval-gated with the *buyer* as approver — the new owner sees every outward action before it happens, which in month one is a feature twice over: control and the fastest possible tour of their own operations |
| **Steady state** (post-close) | Ongoing retainer | Standard delivery loop: weekly iteration, autonomy earned per action on evidence as the buyer's trust and the eval record accrue · the Brain keeps compounding under the new owner · expansion along the normal OS path |

### 3.2 What the buyer holds (the asset split, per the #3 rule)

The Company Brain, handbooks, and captured knowledge are **client-owned artifacts** — the buyer's property, exactly as the Exit-Asset spec draws the line (client-owned artifacts convey; yourco's template/platform layer is licensed under the retainer). A buyer who later cancels keeps the Brain. This is also the honest sales answer to "what if we drop you after transition?" — you keep the knowledge; you lose the operation of it.

### 3.3 Why ETA buyers are the ideal ICP

- **Findable:** they self-identify in structured, enumerable places — search-fund communities, ETA newsletters, MBA ETA programs, buy-side brokerages (channel shape, §6) — unlike the general SMB owner who is findable only one door at a time.
- **Funded:** the deal has committed capital and a transition budget; a transition project priced into a closing is a rounding line on a deal, not a new expense category.
- **AI-curious by selection:** the searcher demographic is young, analytical, and already consuming AI-for-SMB content; no "why AI" education phase.
- **Operationally desperate on a schedule:** the pain has a date certain (closing), maximal intensity (solo owner-operator, day one), and no incumbent solution that survives contact — the ideal Land conditions, purchasable in advance.
- **Repeat + networked:** funds buy multiple companies, and the ETA world is small and loud — one well-run transition is a referral into a community that talks.

**Data sources:** seller-consented operational exhaust (email/calendar/systems, read-only, scoped — the #7 pattern) · structured seller interviews · deal documents the buyer shares for scoping (held under NDA, used for scope only). **Effort band:** M — the machinery is #7's shadow loop + consent pack retargeted at the owner role plus a cutover runbook (~3–5 focused days of template work, most of it shared with Understudy's build); per-engagement Capture is S–M spread across the deal window; Cutover is an intense S burst on a known date.

## 4. Moat fit

- **Trust is the product's precondition, twice:** a *seller* grants pre-close access to their life's work, and a *buyer* trusts agents with live operations in week one — both are grants only an operator with a demonstrated approval/eval/audit posture can receive. No-code tooling cannot be handed either side of this.
- **The autonomy matrix is the transition plan:** R1-everything at cutover is exactly what a nervous new owner wants, and the earned climb maps one-to-one onto their own growing command of the business — the house autonomy standard, sold as the onboarding curve.
- **Compounding asset logic:** every month post-close, the Brain + ledger make the business more documented and less owner-dependent than it was under the seller — the engagement manufactures the Exit-Asset (#3) conditions for the *next* sale. The rail compounds across ownership generations.
- **Model-upgrade dividend:** capture quality and agent capability both appreciate under the fixed retainer — the standing obsolescence answer, applied to a buyer holding a 5–7-year horizon.
- **Flywheel:** Compound / new markets (roadmap coverage map) — with #3, both sides of every small-business sale; each transition also seeds Immune System (#8) density in a new business.

## 5. Gates / compliance

- **Seller consent is a hard precondition** — written, scoped, revocable, with deal-contingent destruction terms (deal dies → capture artifacts destroyed, confirmed in writing). Identical ethic to #7's no-covert-monitoring rule: no capture the seller hasn't signed for, and no capture of *employees'* exhaust without their own per-role consent under the standard #7 pack.
- **Gate #1 scope-rider (engagement legal suite, `processes/counsel-gates.md`):** the transition agreement joins the existing review batch — tri-party consent/access structure (buyer engages yourco; seller grants access pre-close), NDA/confidentiality for pre-close deal context, deal-contingency + artifact-destruction terms, and the client-owned-artifact split (which gate #1 already covers via the #3 rider, 2026-08-06 entry). **No new gate.**
- **Not a deal participant:** yourco takes no role in valuation, diligence conclusions, deal advice, financing, or negotiation — no broker, advisor, or finder posture anywhere (see §8; this boundary is what keeps the offering clear of licensed-activity territory without needing a new gate). Capture artifacts may be *shown* in diligence by the parties; yourco doesn't opine on the deal.
- **Recording posture:** seller interviews follow the existing recording rider on gate #1 (FL two-party consent — explicit consent or written-only capture), per the 2026-08-06 scope entry.
- **launch-gate (`processes/launch-gate.md`; scope row #12):** ETA-channel outreach at scale (newsletter placements, community posts) waits for launch; a *warm* ETA intro is within the gate-12 in-person/unbranded scoping — which is exactly why the roadmap trigger names it as an activation path.
- Heavy-PII and tenant-isolation house standards apply throughout; white-label per house rule — everything staff and customers see carries the business's brand, never yourco's, through both ownerships.

## 6. Pricing frame *(assumption-stated; Polo locks)*

Two components, deliberately shaped like the deal's own cost structure: (1) a **transition project fee** (Capture + Cutover) — fixed-fee, quoted against deal size and complexity, positioned beside the buyer's other closing costs (QoE, legal) where it reads as transition insurance priced like a diligence line; (2) the **standing OS retainer** from day one post-close, on the standard OS-tier bands — the transition converts into a normal mature engagement. Assumption: the project fee sits meaningfully above a single-role Understudy setup (it captures the *whole* owner role and ships day-one operations) and below the buyer's legal bill; searcher-budget reality-checks against real conversations before Polo locks. Deal-contingent billing (capture phase billed as incurred; balance contingent on close) stated up front. No success fees, no percentage-of-deal economics — ever (that's broker compensation territory, §5/§8). All figures illustrative until first-transition evidence.

**Channel shape** (shape, not named outlets, per roadmap): ETA/search-fund communities and their newsletters · MBA ETA programs and their alumni networks · buy-side brokers and SBA-adjacent lenders as referral sources — all warm-intro-first per the 2026-08-05 GTM stance, with the Exit-Asset (#3) engagements themselves as the eventual best channel (every #3 dossier meets a buyer).

## 7. Activation trigger (build)

**First mature engagement OR first warm ETA-community intro, whichever first** — exactly as roadmap row #14. The two triggers reflect the two entry doors: a mature engagement makes the machinery real (a live Brain + #7 artifacts to show a buyer), while a warm intro makes the buyer real (and the machinery can be assembled inside the deal window, since it's mostly #7 retargeted). Pre-trigger work permitted: the consent-pack and transition-agreement drafts into the gate-#1 batch, and the cutover runbook — paper, not build.

## 8. What we will NOT do

- **No covert capture.** No pre-close observation the seller hasn't signed for; no employee exhaust without that employee's own consent; revocation stops capture same-day. We decline the buyer who asks for quiet diligence-by-agent.
- **No deal advice, valuation opinions, or diligence conclusions.** yourco never tells a buyer what the business is worth, whether to close, or what the capture "means for the price." Capture artifacts state what was observed; the deal is the parties' and their advisors'.
- **No success fees or percentage-of-deal pricing** — nothing shaped like broker/finder compensation, in any engagement, under any framing.
- **No day-one autonomy.** Cutover ships R1-everything; the climb is earned on evidence per the standard matrix, and high-stakes actions (payments, pricing commitments, HR) hold at R1 regardless of buyer enthusiasm.
- **No seller-replacement marketing.** The offer is capturing a *departing* owner's knowledge with their consent — never pitched as surveilling or obsoleting a staying team; staff-facing communication about the agents is part of the cutover runbook, not an afterthought.
- **No dual-representation conflicts.** If yourco already serves the seller (an Exit-Asset engagement), the buyer-side engagement is disclosed to both parties and the privacy walls between the two engagements' data are explicit — or we take one side only.
- **No fabricated transition outcomes.** Until real transitions exist, all materials describe mechanism; the first case study waits for a real close and the buyer's written consent.
- **No pre-close operations.** Agents act (even draft) in the business only after close, in the buyer's tenant, under the buyer's authority — pre-close is capture-only, read-only.
