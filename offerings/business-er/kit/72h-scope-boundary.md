# 72h-free stabilization — scope boundary — Business ER (frontier #12)

> **STAGED — internal until launch (OtherVenture) + this offering's own trigger** (post-launch AND first 3–5 white-glove engagements complete). This is the working boundary doc behind the ER short-form terms; the terms themselves ride the gate-#1 counsel batch (best-effort language, liability, rapid-consent access pack, preempt clause, wind-down).

The principle, from SPEC §3.3: free work has a wall clock and a scope fence, both stated at intake, because "free 72 hours" without a fence is a free build with extra steps.

## The clock

- Starts when the case is accepted (T1, within capacity), not when the call comes in.
- **Hour ~48: the fork, in writing.** (a) Convert to a paid engagement — the stopgap hardens into a real module at normal rates; or (b) hand back — we document the stopgap and wind it down cleanly at hour 72. The stabilization report is the client's to keep either way.
- **Hour 72: the window ends.** Fully. Non-converters get the clean wind-down, not a quiet extension.

## In scope (stabilization)

- Triage and the structured assessment.
- A stopgap that keeps the stopped function moving: a temporary intake line, a manual-process bridge doc, an approval-gated draft agent on the golden template — the minimum that stops the bleeding.
- Minimal, revocable, scoped read access under the standard consent pack (rapid-consent version).
- A written stabilization report: what broke, what's holding, what's fragile.
- The full moat layer on anything deployed: R1 approval gates, eval, audit log. Crisis never skips governance (SPEC §8: no autonomous stopgaps; payments, pricing, HR, legal/medical substance stay R1 regardless).

## Out of scope (explicitly, stated at intake)

- A production module or anything built to production depth.
- Integrations beyond the minimum stopgap.
- Anything the client keeps running past hour 72 without converting. Keeping the stopgap is a normal module engagement at normal rates, quoted after the window, never negotiated during it.
- Ongoing operation of any kind.
- New scope mid-window ("while you're in here, could you also…"): the answer is the stability checkpoint, not the free window.
- Work that would degrade a paying engagement — existing clients preempt; the case waits or is declined.

## Zero sales inside the window

During the 72 hours the only conversation is the emergency. No pitch, no upsell, no pricing talk, no "imagine what a full OS could do." The conversion conversation happens once, at the stability checkpoint, where the report's "what's fragile" section speaks for itself — usually toward Understudy (#7) if the emergency was a key person, or a first OS module if it was a process or system. A client who hands back leaves with a clean report, a working wind-down, and a good story; both outcomes feed referral.

## Pricing posture at the fork (per SPEC §6)

No crisis surcharge and no crisis discount. Conversion lands on the standard bands, same as any client. The free window is CAC: tokens + founder hours logged per case so Charles's close shows the channel's true cost.

## What we never promise

Best-effort stabilization, stated as such. No guaranteed response times, no SLAs on stopgaps, no "we'll save your business." And nothing about any case appears in marketing without the client's written consent; otherwise anonymized-pattern learnings only (the #8 rule applied to ER exhaust).
