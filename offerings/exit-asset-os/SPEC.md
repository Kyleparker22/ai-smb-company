# The Exit-Asset OS — Build Spec

**Working name:** The Exit-Asset OS (frontier #3)
**Author:** the Founder
**Stack:** no new runtime — a packaging + contractual layer over artifacts every engagement already produces (SOPs/handbooks, agent specs, eval ledger, integration map) · quarterly compiler loop · diligence pack rendered to PDF/data-room export
**Status:** Spec — see `offerings/_frontier-roadmap.md` row #3. Build trigger: **first mature engagement (~6 months live).**
**Pillar / form factor:** cross-cutting packaging of the whole OS; the deliverable is form factor 3 (a document/data-room surface).

---

## 1. Concept

The engagement, re-framed as what it has quietly become: an **appraisable, transferable operations asset** that raises what the client's business sells for. SMB sale multiples are discounted for one dominant reason — **owner dependence**: the business is worth less because it *is* the owner. Brokers price this directly (owner-run SMBs trade at roughly 2–3.5× SDE; reduced owner-dependence and documented, transferable operations push toward the top of the band — *illustrative industry ranges, not our data, and we say so*). A mature yourco engagement is a machine for manufacturing exactly what that discount punishes the absence of: operations that run without the owner, **documented not as claims but as records** — SOPs and role handbooks (Understudy #7), named agents doing real work, an append-only eval/incident ledger proving the machine runs and self-corrects (#4's data), an integration map, and a handover kit that survives a change of control.

Three deliverables: (1) the **Exit-Asset Dossier** — the standing, quarterly-refreshed package (below); (2) **transferability engineering** — the contractual and technical work that makes the OS actually convey with a sale rather than evaporate at closing; (3) the **buyer-diligence experience** — a data-room-ready pack plus, optionally, live read access to the client console so a buyer's diligence team can watch the operation run. This is the endgame framing of every other frontier offering (roadmap interlocks): the ledger, the understudies, the board minutes are the *evidence exhibits* of owner-independence.

## 2. Why it's never been done

Exit planners and brokers sell the *narrative* of owner-independence — checklists, coaching, a rewritten org chart — because narrative is all a services firm can produce. They cannot produce a system that runs the operations, and software vendors who can produce systems produce *licenses*, which transfer as a contract assignment problem, not as a working operation with a track record. The unlock is an **operated** OS with an append-only proof record: yourco can hand a buyer not a claim ("the owner isn't needed") but a ledger ("here are 14 months of the intake, invoicing, and scheduling functions running with the owner's routine involvement at zero, incidents stated and remediated"). Nobody else has the record because nobody else runs the moat layer. The second unlock is contractual and is yourco's to make unilaterally: **engineering assignability into the engagement agreement from day one**, so the asset is transferable by design rather than by later negotiation. A non-assignable services contract is worth nothing at closing; ours is written to convey.

## 3. Build shape

### 3.1 The Exit-Asset Dossier (compiled quarterly, from existing artifacts)

| Section | Source (already exists in a mature engagement) |
|---|---|
| Operations map — what runs, which pillar, what shape (employee/automation/surface) | engagement docs + module registry |
| Owner-dependence delta — functions moved off the owner, before/after, autonomy tier per action | autonomy matrix history + audit baseline |
| Role continuity — handbooks + understudy coverage per key role | Understudy program (#7) artifacts |
| Reliability record — eval pass history, incidents (zero-or-stated) + remediations, uptime of loops | the eval ledger (#4 schema) |
| Integration map — systems, access model, what disconnects/reconnects at transfer | engagement integration docs |
| Handover kit — credentials-transfer runbook, new-owner onboarding path, 90-day transition plan, yourco continuity terms | written per this spec |
| Cost + terms exhibit — retainer history, what the buyer assumes, assignment mechanics | contract + finance records |

### 3.2 Transferability engineering (the part that must exist before it's needed)

- **Assignability clause** in the standard engagement agreement: client may assign the engagement to an acquirer of the business on notice, retainer terms surviving — drafted into the legal suite *now* (counsel gate #1), because it only has value if it predates the exit by years.
- **Client-owned artifact rule:** handbooks, SOPs, and the client's data/ledger are the client's property (conveyable); yourco's template/platform layer is licensed and continues under the assigned retainer. The line between the two is drawn in the agreement, not argued at closing.
- **Technical transferability:** all client-side access under client-tenant identities (already house practice — the digital employee lives in *their* tenant), so a change of control is an admin handover, not a rebuild.

### 3.3 Buyer-diligence pack

Dossier + raw-ledger export + optionally **live read-only console access for the diligence window** (the single most persuasive artifact: the buyer watches the operation run, incidents and all) + a standing offer of a diligence call answering how the OS transfers. **Effort band:** S–M — dossier compiler ~2–3 days (it's aggregation + render over existing artifacts); assignability drafting rides gate #1; per-client dossier refresh ~hours, quarterly loop.

## 4. Moat fit

The moat, capitalized: proof (the ledger *is* the diligence evidence), trust (extended to a second executive — the buyer), integration (client-tenant depth is what makes transfer an admin task), retention (an engagement positioned as sale-price infrastructure does not get cancelled to save a retainer — this is the strongest churn defense any offering in the roadmap produces), and the model-upgrade dividend restated for a buyer: the asset **appreciates** as models improve, the inverse of every other operations asset in the deal. Flywheel: **Expand** (exit framing justifies the full-OS conversation) and **Compound** twice over — every business sale is a warm handoff to a new owner-operator (the buyer inherits and often extends the engagement), and brokers/M&A advisors who see one clean diligence pack become the highest-intent channel partners on the roster.

## 5. Gates / compliance

- **Not appraisers, brokers, or advisors — stated everywhere.** yourco produces documentation and a working system; it does not value businesses, broker sales, or advise on transactions. All multiple/valuation references on any surface are labeled as third-party industry ranges, illustrative, never a promise of outcome. "Raises the multiple" is the thesis, stated as a hypothesis until real exits provide evidence — and even then, past exits are not promises.
- **No personalized financial or investment advice** (house prohibition, absolute). The dossier informs the client's and buyer's own advisors; deal questions go to their CPA/attorney/broker.
- **Counsel gates:** assignability + IP-ownership split clauses ride the legal-suite review (**gate #1**) — this spec adds those clauses to gate #1's scope explicitly. Buyer data-room access needs an NDA flow (also gate #1 suite). No new standalone gate.
- **Confidentiality/approval:** the dossier contains the client's operational and financial reality — released only on the client's written instruction, to named recipients, logged. Buyer console access is read-only, time-boxed, audit-logged. Client-facing and buyer-facing surfaces white-label per house rules (dossier is co-branded only if the engagement explicitly co-brands; buyer-facing continuity terms obviously name yourco as the counterparty — that's contractual identity, not branding).

## 6. Pricing frame *(assumption-stated; Polo locks)*

Three-part frame: (1) the **standing dossier** as a premium add-on to mature engagements, ~$250–750/mo folded into Operation/Command-level retainers (often included at Command — it's a retention instrument as much as a product); (2) **exit-activation package** when a sale process actually starts — diligence pack, data room, buyer calls, transition runbook: **~$5–15k one-time**, priced against what exit planners charge for narrative alone (~$10–50k, illustrative); (3) the quiet third leg: the **assigned retainer continues with the buyer**, making every client exit a retention event instead of a churn event. All figures illustrative pre-evidence.

## 7. Activation trigger (build)

**First mature engagement, ~6 months live** (roadmap: compounding era, "first renewal conversations") — a dossier needs a record worth showing. Two pieces run early on purpose: the assignability + artifact-ownership clauses enter the legal suite at gate #1 (day one — see §3.2), and the dossier compiler is worth building once the eval ledger (#4) has its first months of data, since it's the same substance re-rendered.

## 8. What we will NOT do

- **No valuation claims or multiple promises.** We never state or imply that a specific business will sell for more, and we never produce a valuation number. Industry ranges quoted on any surface are sourced, dated (12–18-month freshness rule), and labeled illustrative.
- **No brokering, no deal-making, no success fees tied to sale price** — a fee contingent on the client's sale price would make us an interested party in a transaction we claim to merely document (and likely a regulated one; not counsel-tested because we simply won't).
- **No fabricated track record.** Pre-first-exit, all marketing says "designed to be diligence-ready," never "has raised multiples." The first real assigned-retainer exit is the proof event; until then the offering sells on the artifacts a buyer can inspect today.
- **No dossier without a record.** We don't sell exit packaging to a month-2 client — a thin ledger dressed as a dossier is narrative, which is the incumbent product we exist to be distinct from.
- **No release without written client instruction** — the dossier is never shown to a buyer, broker, or anyone else on yourco's initiative, and never used as yourco marketing material without the standard client-approval path (white-label rule; Client Owner precedent).
